Here's what your child learned today:

The 3 Big Rules for being safe around any horse:

  1. Inside voice. Horses are prey animals — sudden loud noises sound like a predator coming to hunt them.
  2. Walk, don't run. Quick movement scares horses. Calm body, calm horse.
  3. Tell the horse you're there. Always say "Hi" before walking near her — especially behind her. Horses have two blind spots (in front of their nose and right behind their tail).

How to read a horse's body:

  • Happy ears point forward. Grumpy ears lie flat back.
  • Calm eyes are soft and blinking. Scared eyes are wide with the white showing.
  • Quiet tail hangs still. Mad tail swishes fast like a fly swatter.

Three warning signs that mean STEP BACK and tell a grown-up:

  1. Ears flat against her head
  2. Tail swishing fast and hard
  3. One back foot lifted off the ground

This is real horse-safety knowledge your child can carry around any animal — at the neighbor's farm, at a 4-H show, at the fair, anywhere — for the rest of their life.

THE PONY POST — Coming to Schools Near You

What is The Pony Post?

The Pony Post is a 4-week classroom reading program built around real ponies, real letters, and real horse-safety skills. We bring a pony to your child's classroom once a week for four weeks. Between visits, the pony mails the class real letters with a hoof-print stamp on every envelope.

Two age tracks:

Pre-K Track (ages 3–5)

  • 25-minute classroom visits
  • 20 new vocabulary words (horse body parts, colors, friendship, opposites)
  • Letter recognition + rhyming + listening comprehension
  • Real animal-safety knowledge
  • A custom book — The Pony Goes to School — delivered by the pony at Visit 2 (every child gets their own copy to take home)

K-2 Track (ages 5–8)

  • 45-minute classroom visits
  • 20 Dolch sight words aligned to Idaho ELA standards (K.RF.3, 1.RF.3, 2.RF.3)
  • Syllable counting with a real pony's hoof (Idaho ELA K.RF.2.b / 1.RF.2)
  • Reading fluency at Visit 3 — kids read aloud to the pony (the pony doesn't judge)
  • The 3 Big Rules + Pony Looks + 3 Warning Signs (full body language curriculum)
  • A custom book delivered by the pony at Visit 2

Every Sprint ends with a Big Day — either a field trip to the ranch in Athol or the Clydesdales visiting the school grounds.

Try Today's Lesson at Home

Ask your child:

  • "What are the 3 Big Rules for being safe around a horse?"
  • "What do happy ears look like? What about grumpy ears?"
  • "Where are a horse's two blind spots?"

If they can answer those three questions, they have real horse-safety knowledge most adults don't have.
