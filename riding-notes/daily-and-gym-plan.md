## Tomorrow — Monday, May 11 (Day 1, Week 1: Reset Module)

No horse. Body work only. ~30–35 minutes at home, on the floor.

### One-time setup (5 min, today only)

- **Scar check.** Sit or stand at a mirror. Run two fingers along your
  tummy-tuck scar. Note where the skin glides freely and where it feels
  stuck. Write it in the notebook with that level of detail
  ("tethered at left near hip / glides fine across center"). This is
  your starting map.
- **Belly photo** straight on, save to a private album. Compare at
  weeks 6 and 12.

### Body work (in this exact order, ~30 min)

**Block 1 — Breath + deep core (12 min)**

1. **Rib-side breathing** — 5 min
   Lie on back, knees bent. One hand on side of ribs, one on belly.
   Breathe so ribs *widen sideways* under your hand. 4 in through the
   nose, 6 out through the mouth.

2. **Pelvic floor connection** — 3 min
   On the exhale, gently lift pelvic floor (like stopping a stream of
   pee, but gentle). Hold 2 sec, fully release. 10 reps.

3. **Deep core re-connect** — 4 min
   Exhale, draw the two front hip points gently *toward each other*
   (side to side, not pulling belly button down). Tiny movement. Hold
   5 sec, release. 10 reps.

**Block 2 — Hip release (5 min)**

4. **Couch stretch** — 90 sec each side
   Back knee against couch/wall, front foot planted. *Tuck tailbone
   under* — that's what stretches the front hip. Don't arch low back.

5. **Figure-4** — 60 sec each side
   On back, knees bent. Right ankle on left thigh. Pull left thigh
   toward chest.

**Block 3 — Hip activate (8 min)**

6. **Glute bridge, squeeze-first** — 3 sets of 8
   Squeeze both glutes hard 2 sec *before* lifting. Then lift. Lower
   slow.

7. **Side-lying clamshell** — 2 sets of 12 each side
   Knees stacked vertical, heels together, open top knee. Don't roll
   the hips back.

8. **Side-lying hip abduction** — 2 sets of 10 each side
   Top leg straight. Lift 12 inches up and slightly *behind* the body.

**Block 4 — Hip load (5 min, every other day — yes on Day 1)**

9. **Bodyweight split squat** — 2 sets of 6 each side
   Lunge stance. Back knee hovers above floor. Push through front heel.

10. **Single-leg glute bridge** — 2 sets of 5 each side
    Bridge with one leg straight. Hold 2 sec at top, hips level.

11. **Wall sit** — 30 sec, 2 reps
    Knees over ankles. Press knees out against a band (or your hands).

### End of day — journal (5 min)

1. What does my body feel like today — joints, lower back, pelvic
   floor, shoulders? Where is the tightness, where is the looseness?
2. What am I afraid of when I think about loping a horse?

### Rules

- Sharp pain = stop. Soreness/burn = fine.
- Water before and after.
- No saddle this week.
- Don't add more. Consistency, not effort.

## GYM ROUTINE — Two days a week, with your husband

**See `gym-rp-framework.md` for the full Renaissance Periodization (Dr.
Mike Israetel) framework — volume landmarks, RIR progression, mesocycle
structure. That file is the canonical source for the gym program.** The
section below is the simplified "what to do at the gym this week" view.

This is the strength foundation for everything else. The home work is
*reconnection and stability*. The gym work is *load* — the deliberate
strengthening that gives you a quiet, powerful seat.

### Ground rules

- **Two days a week.** Tuesday and Friday work well — one day of rest
  before and after each session.
- **45–60 minutes per session.** Do not turn this into 90.
- **No max effort. No jumping. No heavy back squats from the rack.** Your
  joints are not ready and you don't need it.
- **Breathe through everything.** Never hold your breath under load (the
  Valsalva — bracing hard while breath-holding — puts pressure on the
  abdominal wall and the pelvic floor. Both are still rebuilding.
  Exhale on the effort.)
- **Tempo: slow.** 3 seconds down, 1 second pause, 2 seconds up on
  almost everything. Slow is what trains stability.
- **Weight: light to moderate.** You should finish each set with 2-3
  reps still in the tank. If you're grinding the last rep, the weight
  is too heavy for this phase.
- **Husband's role:** spotter, form-checker, partner for the partner
  drills, the person who reminds you not to ego-lift. Buy him a coffee
  after.

### What husband can do alongside you

He'll probably want his own training. The structure below works for
him too — he can do the same lifts at his own weights and rep ranges.
The supersets and partner work are written to be done together. If he
likes lifting heavy, fine — he goes first while you spot, then he
spots you while you go lighter.

### GYM DAY A — Posterior chain + hip (Tuesdays)

**Warm-up (10 min — do together)**

- 5 min easy cardio (bike preferred over treadmill for the knees)
- **Couch stretch** 60 sec each side
- **90/90 hip switches** — sit on the floor, knees bent in 90/90
  position, rotate side to side. 10 reps each direction
- **Glute bridge with squeeze-first** — 1 set of 10 (activates before
  you load)
- **Clamshell with band around knees** — 1 set of 12 each side

**Main lifts (30 min — superset format means do A1 then A2 then rest,
repeat for the sets listed)**

**Superset A** (rest 60 sec between rounds)
- **A1 — Goblet squat** — 3 sets of 8
  Hold one dumbbell vertically at chest. Squat to a depth that feels
  *controlled* — usually thighs parallel to floor, not below. Weight
  in the heels. Knees track over toes. **Exhale on the way up.**
  *Husband:* watches your knees from the side and tells you if they
  cave inward.
- **A2 — Romanian deadlift (RDL) with dumbbells** — 3 sets of 8
  Hold two dumbbells at thighs. Soft knees. Hinge at the hips (push
  butt back, not down). Lower dumbbells along the front of the legs
  to just below the knees. Squeeze glutes to stand. **The work is in
  the hamstrings and glutes, not the back.** If your back rounds,
  stop short.
  *Husband:* this is his lift too — load him heavier.

**Superset B** (rest 60 sec)
- **B1 — Barbell or dumbbell hip thrust** — 3 sets of 10
  Upper back on a bench, feet flat on floor, weight across hips
  (use a pad). Squeeze glutes to lift hips to a line from knees to
  shoulders. Hold 2 sec at the top. **This is the single best lift
  for your seat. Don't skip it.**
  *Husband:* helps you set up the weight, holds the bar steady at
  the start.
- **B2 — Cable or band hip abduction** — 3 sets of 12 each leg
  Standing, cable or loop band around the ankle, leg out to the
  side. Slow. Glute medius work — the muscle that stops you
  collapsing in a turn at the lope.

**Stability + core (8 min — superset)**
- **C1 — Dead bug** — 2 sets of 8 each side
  On back, arms up, knees bent at 90° over hips. Lower opposite arm
  and leg toward floor slowly. Lower back stays glued to floor (this
  is the test). Return slow.
- **C2 — Pallof press** — 2 sets of 10 each side
  Stand sideways to a cable or band at chest height. Hold the handle
  at the chest with both hands. Press straight out, hold 2 sec,
  return. The band tries to twist you — you resist. **Anti-rotation
  core. Best core lift for riders.**

**Finisher (5 min, optional, do with husband)**
- **Farmer's carry / suitcase carry** — 4 walks of 30 seconds, alternating sides
  Hold one dumbbell at your side (suitcase = one side only). Walk
  slow. Keep both shoulders level — don't let the loaded side hike or
  drop. This is a full-body stability test and it carries straight
  over to staying square in the saddle.
  *Husband:* walks with you carrying his own.

**Cool-down (5 min)**
- Couch stretch 90 sec each side
- Figure-4 stretch 60 sec each side
- 2 minutes of slow rib-side breathing on the floor

### GYM DAY B — Single leg + stability (Fridays)

**Warm-up (10 min — same as Day A)**

**Main lifts (30 min)**

**Superset A** (rest 60 sec)
- **A1 — Step-up onto a 12-inch box or bench** — 3 sets of 6 each leg
  One foot planted on box. Drive through the heel to stand up tall.
  Lower the other foot slowly back to the floor. **Do not push off
  the floor foot** — all the work is the planted leg. This is mounting
  practice and lope practice in one.
- **A2 — Bulgarian split squat** — 3 sets of 6 each leg
  Back foot elevated on a bench behind you, front foot a stride
  forward. Lower the back knee toward the floor. Push through front
  heel to stand. **Light weight or bodyweight for the first month.**
  Single-leg stability gold.

**Superset B** (rest 60 sec)
- **B1 — Single-leg glute bridge** — 3 sets of 6 each side
  Same as the home version, but pause 3 sec at the top. Hips level.
- **B2 — Lat pulldown or seated row** — 3 sets of 10
  This is for posture. The shoulders-back / sternum-up posture you
  need in the saddle starts with the back muscles. Light weight,
  slow tempo, squeeze the shoulder blades.

**Stability + core (8 min)**
- **C1 — Bird dog** — 2 sets of 8 each side
  Hands and knees. Extend opposite arm + leg. Hold 3 sec. Lower slow.
  Don't twist the hips.
- **C2 — Wall sit with banded knees** — 2 sets of 45 sec
  Press knees out against the band. The whole time.

**Partner finisher (5 min)**
- **Banded clamshell hold** — 30 sec each side
  Band around the knees. Open top knee to clamshell position and
  *hold*. Burn city.
- **Back-to-back wall sit with husband** — 45 sec, 2 rounds
  Sit against each other for the wall. Backs touching, both hold the
  position. Whoever drops first buys dinner.

**Cool-down (5 min)** — same as Day A

## How the gym connects to riding

| Gym lift | What it gives you in the saddle |
|---|---|
| Goblet squat | The leg position. Heels down, knees bent, back straight = stirrup stance |
| Romanian deadlift | The hip hinge. Trains the glutes/hamstrings that hold your seat against the lope's push |
| Hip thrust | Glute power. The single most important muscle group for the seat |
| Hip abduction | Glute medius. The muscle that stops you collapsing inward on a turn |
| Step-up | Mounting. Loping push-off. Single-leg stability under load |
| Bulgarian split squat | Asymmetry correction. The weak side gets called out |
| Pallof press | Anti-rotation core. The body's resistance to the horse twisting you |
| Suitcase carry | Staying square in the saddle. Side-to-side stability |
| Bird dog | The "diagonal" stability the lope demands (cross-body coordination) |
| Lat pulldown / row | Shoulders back, chest open, breath free. Riding posture |

Nothing in this routine is random. Every lift earns its place.

## Progression rules

- **Weeks 1–4:** stay light. Goal is movement quality. If you can't do
  the tempo, the weight is too heavy.
- **Weeks 5–8:** add 5 lbs to the lifts where the last 2 reps still
  felt easy. Hold everything else.
- **Weeks 9–12:** start adding a third set to one or two lifts. Don't
  add weight *and* sets the same week.
- **Anytime joints flare:** drop weight 30%, hold for a week, retest.

---

## Red flags — stop and message a PT if you get any of these

- Doming or bulging in the belly during a lift (especially around the
  scar)
- Sharp pain in the SI joint (one side of the lower back, near the dimple)
- Knee pain that lingers more than a day
- Pelvic floor heaviness, leakage, or pressure
- Lower back that takes more than a day to settle
- Scar that becomes red, hot, or starts pulling unevenly

None of these are emergencies, but all of them are signals to slow
down and check in with a professional. The 12-week timeline is a
guideline. Your body sets the real pace.

## What "good" looks like at the end of week 4

- You can do all the home work without thinking about the order
- Bulgarian split squats are no longer wobbly on either side
- The hip thrust feels like glutes, not lower back
- Scar tissue is gliding better than week 1 (or you've got the PT
  appointment booked)
- You can sit on a hard chair and find both seat bones in under 5 seconds
- The notebook has 28 dated entries
- You haven't lost interest

If most of those are true, you're ready for Module 2 (the walking
horse). If they're not, run Module 1 for two more weeks. Nobody's
counting.
