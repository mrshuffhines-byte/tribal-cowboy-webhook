# Tribal Cowboy Pony, What Do You See?
## A Presentation Book for The Pony Post — Visit 1
### Tribal Cowboy LLC — Athol, Idaho

**What this is:** Stacie's own version of the call-and-response picture book format made famous by *Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?* (Bill Martin Jr. / Eric Carle, Henry Holt, 1967). This is an **original work** that uses the public-domain rhyme pattern with Stacie's own text, her own ranch animals, and her own photos. **Do not replicate Eric Carle's exact text or use his illustrations** — that would be infringement. This is a separate, branded Tribal Cowboy book.

**How it's used:** Stacie holds it up at Visit 1 of every Pony Post Sprint (both Pre-K and K-2 tracks). She also uses it at demo visits and at family events at the ranch. It is **not** a take-home book — only 1–2 copies exist, and they live in Stacie's truck.

**Replaces:** The *Brown Bear, Brown Bear (substitute pony)* read-aloud at Visit 1.

| Use case | Why |

---

## The Format

- **Size:** 12x12 inches (square) OR 11x14 inches (portrait). Square is easier to lay out and more iconic.
- **Page count:** 20 pages (10 spreads) — cover, 9 animal spreads, back cover
- **Binding:** Hardcover for durability (kids touch presentation books at every visit) OR saddle-stitched for cheaper start
- **Paper:** Heavy gloss or matte cardstock — survives kid handling
- **Production:** Lulu, Blurb, or a local print shop. Amazon KDP doesn't do oversized hardcover well.

---

## The Animal Sequence

Build from small/familiar (pony) to big/wow (Clydesdale Abby). Mirrors the Sprint's emotional arc — meet the pony at Visit 1, meet Abby at Visit 4. Kids see Abby in the book at Visit 1 and spend 3 weeks knowing she's coming.

| Spread | Animal | Photo direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tribal Cowboy Pony** | Wide head-and-shoulders shot of the pony, ranch backdrop |
| 2 | **Brown Goat** | The goat at the ranch, alert ears |
| 3 | **Orange Barn Cat** | Cat sitting on a hay bale, looking at camera |
| 4 | **White Rooster** | Rooster mid-crow or alert, fence backdrop |
| 5 | **Black-and-White Dog** | Ranch dog, ears up |
| 6 | **Brown Cow** | Side profile of a cow at the fence (or substitute another large animal) |
| 7 | **Spotted Pony** (or Appaloosa) | A second pony with markings for visual variety |
| 8 | **Huge Clydesdale** | **Abby.** Full body shot showing scale. The BIG reveal. |
| 9 | **A whole field of friends** | Wide ranch shot with multiple animals in frame |
| 10 | **Kids!** | Backs-of-heads shot of kids at a visit (no faces — photo-release safe) |

If you don't have every animal at the ranch, swap freely. Don't force a sequence — substitute in what you actually own.

## Why This Sequence Works for Pre-K and K-2

| Skill | How the book teaches it |
|---|---|
| **Vocabulary** | 9 animal names + 9 colors. Most words are 1–2 syllables. |
| **Color recognition** | Every animal is described by color (brown, orange, white, black-and-white, spotted, huge) |
| **Phonological awareness** | The rhyme/repetition structure trains predictive listening — by spread 3 kids start chanting the question with you |
| **Print awareness** | Big text, one sentence per page, left-to-right reading |
| **Listening comprehension** | The pattern lets kids predict what's coming — they read the book "with" you |
| **Syllable counting** | Most animals are 1–3 syllables. Tie back to the Pony Stomps moment. *"How many beats? Spot-ted po-ny. Let's stomp it."* |
| **Pre-counting** | "I see ONE goat. I see ONE cat." Optional — fits Pre-K counting work |
| **Story arc** | Builds from small to big, climaxes at Abby, points the lens at the audience |

## Production Path

### Step 1 — Take the photos (1 weekend)

Take 12+ photos of ranch animals. For each animal:
- Full-body or head-and-shoulders shot
- Animal facing the camera or looking toward it (looking AT the viewer is the magic of these books)
- Plain background where possible — fences, hay, sky
- Same lighting condition across all photos if possible (morning or early evening)

If you don't have every animal at the ranch:
- Substitute with what you have (a duck instead of a rooster, a barn cat is universal)
- Or borrow — most ranch friends will let you photograph their goat
- Don't use stock photos — kids will sense the inauthenticity

### Step 2 — Lay out the book in Canva (1 weekend)

- Canva Pro account: $13/month (cancel after layout)
- **Template:** Children's Picture Book, 12x12 size (not the small format)
- **Font:** Bold, friendly, large — Quicksand, Fredoka, or Caveat at 60-80pt for the text
- **Layout per spread:** One photo on the left page (full bleed), text on the right page in big bold type
- **Title page:** Big photo + title in bold serif font like Playfair Display
- **Back cover:** Smaller text + Tribal Cowboy logo + QR

### Step 3 — Print

**Recommended:** Lulu Press or Blurb for the hardcover oversized format. KDP doesn't do 12x12 hardcover well.

| Vendor | Format | Cost per book | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lulu** | 12x12 hardcover, 20 pages, full color | $22–$28 | 7–10 days |
| **Blurb** | Premium photo book, 12x12 hardcover | $35–$55 | 5–7 days |
| **Local print shop** | 12x12 saddle-stitched booklet | $5–$10 | 3–5 days |

**Recommendation:** Order ONE hardcover from Lulu first (~$25). Test it for a few visits. If it survives, order a backup copy. If it tears, downgrade to saddle-stitched booklet from a local printer (cheaper, easier to replace when worn).

### Step 4 — Test before the first official Sprint

- Read it to 1–3 kids at home (your own kids, niece/nephew, friend's kids)
- Watch the pacing. Do they sit through the whole book?
- Watch the energy. Where do they laugh? Where do they zone out?
- Adjust the sequence if needed. Animals can be reordered.
