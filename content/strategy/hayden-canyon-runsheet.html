Critical Setup — Animals Stay, Classes Rotate

You CANNOT walk three animals from classroom to classroom. They stay in ONE location all day. Classes come to them.

Best location: Gym OR outdoor covered area. Avoid carpet. Lay stall mats or tarps on hard floors.

The setup:

  • Pony, horse, and Clydesdale in their own marked-off areas (15+ feet apart so kids can see scale)
  • Hay nets out of kid sight (let animals snack between sessions)
  • Water buckets behind each animal
  • Quiet zone rope 6+ feet from each animal
  • Materials table at the entrance: take-home one-pagers, business cards, Sprint flyer

The HELPER: You need ONE adult helper for the day. Their job: watch each animal for stress, manage materials, hand out water/hay, take photos (no kid faces unless releases signed), take notes on kid + teacher quotes.

Animal Welfare Protocol (the non-negotiable)

Watch for stress signals on EACH animal: tail swishing more than usual, ears pinned back, lifting feet anxiously, refusing treats or water, heavy sweating in cool air, sudden head-tossing or backing up.

If any animal shows signs: cut their feature time, other animals carry the session, 30 min in a quiet space, water + hay.

Required breaks: 20 min after 2nd Grade, 30 min for lunch, 5 min between every session.

Universal Opening — Use at the START of every session

Adjust energy by age but keep the structure. About 3 minutes.

"Hi everybody. I'm Stacie. I'm a horse person from a place called Tribal Cowboy in Athol. Today I brought my pony, my horse, and my Clydesdales to meet you. The Clydesdales are the biggest horses you'll ever see.

Before anybody does anything, we have THREE rules. Just three. Hold up three fingers like me.

RULE ONE: Inside voice. No screaming. Horses are prey animals — a scream sounds like a wolf coming to hunt them.
RULE TWO: Walk, don't run. Quick moving things scare horses.
RULE THREE: If you walk near a horse, say HI first — especially behind her. Horses have blind spots.

Three rules. Inside voice. Walk. Say hi. Got it?

Let me also teach you ONE quick signal. When I say 'WHOA, PARTNERS!' you FREEZE and put your hands behind your ears like this (demo Pony Ears). We'll use that any time it gets loud."

10:00–10:25 — Reese & AM Kinder (42 kids) | 2:00–2:25 — PM Kinder (16 kids)

Theme: Meet the three animals. Learn to be gentle.

  • 0:00–0:03 Universal opening
  • 0:03–0:08 Pony body parts. Point: hoof, mane, tail, eye. Kids touch their own. Count her hooves (1, 2, 3, 4).
  • 0:08–0:13 Meet the horse. "Bigger than a pony. Same parts, just bigger. Watch her ears."
  • 0:13–0:20 Meet the Clydesdales. "Biggest horses you'll ever see. Look at their HOOVES — bigger than a dinner plate. Each one eats 25 pounds of hay every day." Show a hoof up close.
  • 0:20–0:23 Sensory wrap. Each kid waves to one animal of their choice.
  • 0:23–0:25 Goodbye + sticker handout.

Don't: Don't let 5-year-olds approach the Clydesdales unsupervised. Helper holds each halter the whole time.
