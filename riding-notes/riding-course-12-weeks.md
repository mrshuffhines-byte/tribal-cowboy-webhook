# A 12-Week Course to Relearn the Horse — Built for Your Body

For Stacie. 34, post-kids, weak joints, training a quiet independent seat
in a western saddle. Builds on `western-saddle-relearn.md`.

This is a real course, not just a list of tips. Print it. Mark it up. Track
your weeks. Move slower if the body says so.

---

## What's different about your body, and why this course is designed this way

Three realities are shaping the whole program:

**1. You've had babies.** Relaxin (the hormone that loosens ligaments during
pregnancy) can linger for 6+ months after weaning. Your joints may still feel
"loopy" — that's not weakness, that's laxity. The fix is *stability through
strength*, not stretching. Pregnancy can also leave the linea alba (the front
seam of the abs) widened — **diastasis recti** — which means crunches and
front-loaded core work are off the table until you've checked and rehabbed it.

**2. Weak joints.** For most women this means knees, ankles, SI joint
(lower back / pelvis), and sometimes wrists. The course uses **isometrics
and slow tempo** instead of plyometrics. We build joint stability before
we ask the joints to absorb a 1000-lb horse's motion.

**3. You're a beginner-rebooting, not a beginner.** Your body has riding
memory but it also has compensations — gripping with the thigh, balancing
on the rein, holding the breath at the lope. Half the work is unlearning.

**The program respects all three. It is slow on purpose.**

---

## Before you start — five things to do this week

1. **Check for diastasis recti.** Lie on your back, knees bent. Lift your head
   and look at your belly. If you see a vertical "ridge" or "trough" down the
   midline wider than two fingers, you have some separation. Don't panic —
   it's super common after kids. But skip front-loaded core work (sit-ups,
   crunches, planks) until you've worked with a pelvic floor PT. Replace
   with the work in Week 1.
2. **Find a pelvic floor PT** if you can. One session is worth ten YouTube
   videos. Many take insurance now. North Idaho options exist — ask your
   OB for a referral.
3. **Pick the right horse.** For this whole 12 weeks, ride one horse that
   you trust. Ideally a well-trained, calm, confident horse who knows the
   lope and won't take advantage of a quiet rider. Switching horses every
   ride wrecks the relearning.
4. **Get the right gear.**
   - Western saddle that fits *you* (not just the horse). The seat width
     should let both your seat bones contact, with about a hand's width of
     room in front. A seat that's too big is as bad as one that's too small.
   - Stirrups level. Have someone check from the ground.
   - A helmet, every ride, regardless of what the cowboy culture says.
     Brain stays put.
5. **Set up the camera.** Phone, tripod, $20 on Amazon. Every ride, two
   angles: side and behind. You will rewatch.

## The course at a glance

| Weeks | Module | Focus | Where you ride |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | **Reset** | Body audit, breath, diastasis check, ground-only | Off-horse + groundwork |
| 3–4 | **Foundation** | Mount, halt, walk only. Find seat bones. | Halt and walk, arena |
| 5–6 | **Walk mastery** | Independent seat at walk. Drop the reins. | Walk only |
| 7–8 | **Trot return** | Posting trot, sitting trot in short bursts | Walk + trot |
| 9–10 | **Sitting trot + transitions** | The trot is the lope's classroom | Walk + trot |
| 11 | **First lopes** | 3-stride lopes, halt. Confidence first. | Walk + trot + 3-stride lopes |
| 12 | **Lope a circle** | The graduation week | All three gaits |

If a week feels rushed, repeat it. The schedule is a guide, not a deadline.

---

## Weekly format (read once, then follow it every week)

Each week has the same structure:

- **Learning goal** — one sentence
- **Body work** — what to do off-horse, daily
- **Saddle time** — what to do on-horse, 2–3x this week
- **Body check** — what to watch for in your own body
- **Journal prompts** — two questions to answer in writing

Keep one notebook for the whole course. Date every entry. You're going to
forget how week 1 felt by week 8, and the journal is how you measure
progress when the wins are small.

# MODULE 1 — RESET (Weeks 1–2)

You don't ride this module. You rebuild the operating system underneath
the riding.

## Week 1 — Audit and breathe

**Learning goal:** Know what your body is starting with. Wake up the breath
and the deep core (transverse abdominis + pelvic floor).

**Body work (daily, 15 min):**

- **Diaphragmatic breathing** (5 min)
  Lie on back, knees bent, one hand on chest, one on belly. Breathe so only
  the belly hand rises. 4 in through the nose, 6 out through the mouth.
  This is your nervous-system reset and your core foundation.

- **Pelvic floor connection** (3 min)
  Same position. On the exhale, lightly draw the pelvic floor up — the
  feeling of stopping a stream of pee, but **gentle**. Hold 2 seconds.
  Release fully (release matters as much as engage). 10 reps. Stop if you
  feel doming/bulging in your belly.

- **Transverse abdominis activation** (3 min)
  Same position. Exhale, draw belly button gently toward spine. Like
  hugging the spine from the inside. Hold 5 seconds. 10 reps.

- **Glute bridge (joint-friendly version)** (2 min)
  Knees bent, feet flat. Exhale, draw the deep core in, lift hips only
  to a line from knees to shoulders (don't over-arch). Hold 3 seconds at
  the top. 10 reps.

- **Side-lying clamshell** (2 min, each side)
  On your side, knees bent and stacked. Heels together, open the top knee
  like a clam. Slow. 12 reps. Stops you collapsing to one side later.

**Saddle time:** None. Resist the urge.

**Body check:**
- Diastasis test (described above). Photo of belly straight on. Save it.
- Can you breathe into your belly without your shoulders rising?
- Can you find your two seat bones sitting on a hard chair? (Section 3 of
  the other guide.)

**Journal prompts:**
1. What does my body feel like today — joints, lower back, pelvic floor,
   shoulders? Where is the tightness, where is the looseness?
2. What am I afraid of when I think about loping a horse? (Be honest.
   Write the actual sentences.)

---

## Week 2 — Mobility within stability

**Learning goal:** Open the hips and pelvis *without* over-stretching loose
joints.

**Body work (daily, 20 min):**

- All of week 1 (15 min)
- Add: **90/90 hip rotation** (3 min)
  Sit on the floor, one leg bent in front at 90°, other to the side at
  90°. Lean forward, hold 30 seconds. Switch sides. Switch legs (so the
  other leg is in front), repeat.
- Add: **Cat / cow** on hands and knees (2 min)
  Slow. Move only as far as feels controlled — not the deepest range.
  This is for *pelvic awareness*, not the deepest stretch.

**Saddle time:** Groundwork only.
- Spend two sessions just being with your horse. Grooming, leading,
  desensitizing. Reset the relationship before you reset the ride.
- Stand at the side of the horse, hand on the saddle. Practice your
  mounting plan: from a mounting block (always use one — saves your knees
  and the horse's spine), one foot in stirrup, swing the leg over slowly,
  sit down softly. Repeat the mount and dismount five times each session.
  No riding yet.

**Body check:**
- After the hip work, is your low back / SI joint OK or angry?
- Are your knees clicking? (Common — note it, don't ignore it.)
- Can you mount without using your arms to haul yourself up? Use the
  mounting block higher if not.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What is one tight spot and one loose spot in my body? (The strategy is
   different for each.)
2. What do I want from riding? Trail confidence? Photography for the
   business? Loping a circle without fear? Name the actual goal.

# MODULE 2 — FOUNDATION (Weeks 3–6)

The walk. All of it. Don't underestimate this module.

## Week 3 — Halt and walk, no reins

**Learning goal:** Find the two seat bones on a moving horse.

**Body work (daily, 20 min):**
- Continue week 2 work.
- Add: **Single-leg balance** (1 min each side)
  Stand on one leg, soft knee, find a focal point. Don't lock the knee.
  This is your stirrup leg.

**Saddle time (2 rides this week, 20 min each):**

- *Ride 1:*
  - Mount. Sit at the halt for 2 minutes. Eyes closed. Feel the two seat
    bones. Breath: 4 in, 6 out.
  - On a lead rope (have a helper lead the horse), walk one full lap of the
    arena. Reins draped on the horn. Hands on your thighs.
  - Halt. Re-find the seat bones. Walk another lap.
  - 4 laps total. End.

- *Ride 2:*
  - Same warm-up.
  - Walk with reins, but soft contact. Hands quiet on the horn or thighs.
  - Practice transitions: halt → walk → halt → walk. Each transition,
    re-find the seat bones first.
  - 15 minutes of walk and halt.

**Body check:**
- At the walk, can you feel the horse's hips swinging your seat bones
  left-right-left-right? (You should — that's the walk's four-beat in
  your pelvis.)
- Lower back: collapsed (chair seat) or arched (anterior)? Camera answers.
- Are you gripping with the thigh? Open the knee one inch.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What did the walk feel like in my body today — was anything new?
2. Where was my breath when the horse moved off?

---

## Week 4 — Walk with intention

**Learning goal:** The walk is a tool. Practice using the seat to influence
the walk, not just survive it.

**Body work (daily, 20 min):** Same as week 3.

**Saddle time (3 rides, 20–25 min):**

- Walk-halt transitions: use your **breath and seat** before the rein.
  - To halt: exhale, sit deep, both seat bones drop, *then* rein.
  - To walk: inhale, open hip angle slightly, leg cue, soften the hand.
- Walk in figures: 20-meter circle, change rein across diagonal, walk a
  serpentine. (Western arenas often have cones — use them.)
- 10 minutes of one-handed walking. Other hand on the horn. Feel where
  you *want* to grab for balance.

**Body check:**
- Are your shoulders level? (Camera from behind.)
- Is the saddle drifting left or right? (That's your asymmetry showing.)
- Is your rein hand steady or bouncing? (Bouncing = balancing on the rein.)

**Journal prompts:**
1. Did I find the seat bones today within the first minute, or did it take
   the whole ride?
2. What's one thing I'm doing better than week 1?

---

## Week 5 — Drop the reins

**Learning goal:** Trust the seat enough to ride without a rein hand.

**Body work (daily, 20 min):** Same.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- 10 minutes of bareback-style walking *in the saddle* — both hands on
  the horn or saddle pommel, reins draped. (Don't go actually bareback
  yet, even though it's a great drill, it's hard on the joints when
  you're rebuilding.)
- 5 minutes of **eyes-closed walking**, on a safe horse, in a safe arena.
  Hands on the horn. Eyes closed for 10 steps at a time. Feel the four-beat.
- Walk-halt transitions: now without reaching for the rein. Voice + seat.

**Body check:**
- Without the rein for balance, do you slump forward? Lift your sternum.
- Are you breathing? Record audio. Listen back.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What did "no reins" reveal about my balance?
2. What is one fear-thought I let go of this week?

---

## Week 6 — Walk mastery

**Learning goal:** A neutral, quiet, independent seat at the walk in any
direction. This is the floor of everything else.

**Body work (daily, 25 min):**
- Same work. Add **glute bridge with marching** (alternate lifting one foot
  off the floor while holding the bridge): 2 sets of 8 each leg. Trains
  one-sided pelvic stability.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- Walk full ride. 30 minutes per session.
- Drills: stirrup-stand at the walk (stand in stirrups, heels deep, knees
  soft, upper body erect, 10 strides). Sit. Repeat 5x.
- One-handed walk in both directions, 5 minutes each direction.
- Eyes-closed walking — extend to 20 steps.

**Body check:**
- Can you hold a soft three-point seat (two seat bones + light pubic
  contact) without thinking?
- Is your right side and left side equal? (Most people are 60/40. Aim
  for 55/45 by the end of the course.)

**Journal prompts:**
1. If a beginner watched me walk this horse, what would they see?
2. Am I ready for the trot? (Honest answer.)

# MODULE 3 — BUILDING (Weeks 7–10)

The trot. Where most of the seat actually gets built.

## Week 7 — Posting trot

**Learning goal:** Rise and sit in rhythm with the horse. Energy comes from
the leg and hip, not the knee.

**Body work (daily, 25 min):**
- Same. Add: **wall-sit** with knees in line with ankles, 3x 20 seconds.
  Builds the quad endurance you need for the posting trot without
  beating up the knees.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- 10 min walk warm-up. Drills from module 2.
- Posting trot, short stretches: 10 strides, walk. 10 strides, walk.
- Build to 20 strides, then a full long side, then a full lap.
- Rest between sets — your inner thighs and hip flexors will tell you when.

**Body check:**
- Are you rising from your **thigh and hip** (good) or from your **knee
  and stirrup** (bad — that's chair-seat country)?
- Are your hands quiet when you rise and sit? (They should not pump.)
- Lower back OK? If it hurts, you're using your back instead of your
  glutes to rise. Glute-bridge work matters here.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What does the posting trot feel like in my hips today?
2. Where is the fatigue — in the "right" places (glutes, inner thigh) or
   "wrong" (lower back, knees)?

---

## Week 8 — Sitting trot in short bursts

**Learning goal:** Sit a few strides of trot. This is the lope's classroom.

**Body work (daily, 25 min):**
- Same. Add: **bird-dog** on hands and knees, opposite arm + leg extended,
  hold 5 sec. 2 sets of 6 each side. Trains the cross-body coordination
  you need at the lope.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- Warm up walk → posting trot.
- **Sit two strides, post two strides.** Repeat for a full long side.
- Build to sit 4 / post 4. Then sit 6.
- Keep it short. Sitting the trot is hard on weak joints — small doses,
  many reps.
- Use the horn if you need to. Better one hand on the horn than a
  tensed seat.

**Body check:**
- When you sit, does your pelvis rock with the horse (good) or stay locked
  (bad — that's what causes bouncing)?
- Are you holding your breath when you sit? Almost everyone does. Force the
  exhale.

**Journal prompts:**
1. How many strides of sitting trot before my body started gripping?
2. What did I notice in my pelvis when I sat the trot today?

---

## Week 9 — Transitions, transitions, transitions

**Learning goal:** Move smoothly between walk-trot-walk without losing the
seat. The lope is just bigger transitions — get good at small ones first.

**Body work (daily, 25 min):** Same.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- The whole ride is transitions.
- Walk → trot → walk every 10 strides.
- Then every 6 strides.
- Then every 4 strides.
- End with one figure-eight at the trot, sitting the trot through the
  middle (just 3-4 strides).
- Use breath and seat *before* hand for every transition. To trot:
  inhale and engage the seat. To walk: exhale and sit deeper.

**Body check:**
- Is your transition smooth or jerky? (Camera audio: listen for the moment
  the horse shifts — your body should not lurch.)
- Are you using the rein to slow down (bad habit) or your seat (the goal)?

**Journal prompts:**
1. What does it feel like to slow a horse down with my seat alone?
2. What's my biggest hang-up about going to the lope?

---

## Week 10 — Long sitting trot

**Learning goal:** Sit the trot for half a long side without bouncing.

**Body work (daily, 30 min):** Same plus an extra 5 min of hip mobility.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- Build sitting trot up to half a long side, then a full long side.
- Stay on a quiet horse — don't push it to a big trot. A *slow*, *swinging*
  jog is what you want.
- 5 min of bareback walk if your back and the horse can tolerate it. (One
  ride only — see how it feels.) This is where the seat bones become
  permanent in your nervous system.

**Body check:**
- Are you still smiling at the end? If you're white-knuckled, slow down.
- Hips loose? Lower back quiet?

**Journal prompts:**
1. Could I do this same trot work for a guest at a Tribal Cowboy event and
   look confident?
2. What's the smallest amount of lope I'd be willing to try next week?

# MODULE 4 — THE LOPE (Weeks 11–12)

You've earned it.

## Week 11 — First lopes

**Learning goal:** 3 strides of lope, halt softly. Repeat. No drama.

**Body work (daily, 30 min):** Same. Plus: **pelvic floor + diaphragm
breathing for 5 min before every ride.** This will save you when the
lope speeds up your heart rate.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

Use the **lope-prep checklist** from `western-saddle-relearn.md`,
Section 6, every single time.

- *Ride 1:*
  - Warm up 15 min, walk + trot.
  - One lope, 3 strides, then trot.
  - Walk. Breathe. Pat the horse.
  - One more lope, 3 strides.
  - That's it. Two lopes. Done.

- *Ride 2:*
  - 4 lopes of 3-4 strides each, both directions.

- *Ride 3:*
  - 4 lopes of 5 strides each, both directions.
  - End with a walk and an open hand on the horse's neck.

**Body check:**
- Are you breathing? Audio check.
- Are your shoulders pinned back to your ears (fear) or sitting calmly
  over your hips (good)?
- Did your seat stay in the saddle, or did you come up?
- Is the lope getting *easier* by ride 3? It should.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What was the fear like before the first lope? After?
2. What surprised me about the lope this week?

---

## Week 12 — Lope a circle

**Learning goal:** Lope one circle, both directions, breathing the whole way.

**Body work (daily, 30 min):** Same.

**Saddle time (3 rides):**

- *Ride 1:*
  - Build from 5 strides → half a circle → three-quarter circle, in your
    *easy* direction.
  - End on a good one. Don't push for more.

- *Ride 2:*
  - Half circles in both directions.
  - Then a full circle in the easy direction.

- *Ride 3:*
  - Full circle, both directions.
  - End with a long walk on a loose rein.

**Body check:**
- On the harder direction, where is your asymmetry showing? (Most likely
  you're collapsing to the inside.)
- Did you trust the seat or grab the horn?
- Was there a single stride that felt *good*? Mark it. That's the
  feeling you're chasing.

**Journal prompts:**
1. What did the lope feel like in my body today vs. day one of this course?
2. What's the next goal?

## Graduation — what to do after week 12

You're not "done." You've just built the floor.

- Keep doing the daily 20-30 min body work — non-negotiable. The whole
  course rests on this and it doesn't end.
- Ride twice a week minimum. The seat is a muscle memory; muscle memory
  goes stale fast.
- Add one lope ride a week where you push slightly past your comfort:
  a longer circle, a small straight stretch, a slightly faster lope. Tiny
  increments.
- Revisit `western-saddle-relearn.md` — read it again, you'll see new
  things now.
- Find a coach. One in-person lesson a month is worth a year of solo
  practice. Look for someone who teaches **balanced western horsemanship**
  — names to ask about in the region: Julie Goodnight clinicians, Buck
  Brannaman students, or a local CHA (Certified Horsemanship Association)
  instructor.

## Safety rules that don't change

1. **Helmet every ride.** Period.
2. **Mounting block every time.** Saves your hips, knees, and the horse's
   spine.
3. **One horse for the whole course.** Switch only if the horse is the
   problem.
4. **No riding alone for module 4.** Have someone in the arena, in eye
   contact, with a phone.
5. **Stop the ride if your joints are talking loud.** Knees, SI joint,
   pelvic floor. The 12-week timeline is a guideline. Your body sets the
   real pace.
6. **Cycle days, post-baby healing days, exhaustion days.** Skip the ride,
   keep the 5-minute breath work. Don't ride tired. Riding tired is how
   the bad habits come back.

## A short note on the post-baby body

A lot of riding instruction was written for 22-year-olds who haven't
carried a child. The body you're riding in now is *better* in some ways —
it's smarter, more aware, more patient — and **different** in others. The
pelvic floor, the deep core, the SI joint, the hip mobility — these are
things you have to actively build, not assume you have.

That's not a weakness. That's just biology. The riders who treat their
body honestly tend to ride better and longer than the ones who don't.

Pegasus Physio's whole catalog is essentially written for adult riders
returning to the saddle. Lean on it.
