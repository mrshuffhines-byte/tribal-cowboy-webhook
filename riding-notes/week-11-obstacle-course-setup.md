# Week 11 — Home Obstacle Course Setup Guide

Companion to `week-11-obstacle-course.svg`. Read once, then take the SVG (or a printed copy) to the arena.

---

## What you need

| Item | Quantity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ground poles (8-10' long) | 8-10 | Can be PVC, wood, or actual horse poles. Lumberyard 2×4s work. |
| Traffic cones | 5-7 | The orange road-construction kind. Tractor Supply sells them. |
| Tarp | 1 | 6' × 8' minimum. Blue tarp from any hardware store. |
| Sandbags or rocks | 4 | To weight the tarp corners so it doesn't flap. |
| Gate posts | 2 | Existing arena gate works. Or two T-posts pounded in 12' apart. |
| Latch/lever | 1 | If using a real gate. A rope-with-loop works for a fake gate. |
| Optional: tape measure | 1 | For accurate first-time setup. |

**Total cost if buying everything new:** ~$120-180. Most of it is one-time.

---

## Suggested arena size

The diagram is laid out for an 80' × 60' arena, which is the smallest practical western arena. **Scale to your space:**

- Bigger arena → spread stations further apart, give yourself more transition room
- Smaller arena → compress the layout, but **never compress the actual obstacles** (a 4' weave gap isn't a 4' weave gap if you make it 2')

If you can't fit all 7 stations comfortably, **do 3-4 stations per ride** instead. The point isn't to cram, it's to ride each obstacle well.

## Station-by-station setup

### Station 1 — Walk-Over Poles
- 3 poles, parallel, on the ground
- **Spacing: 4 feet between poles** (measured pole-to-pole, not edge-to-edge)
- Poles 10 feet long
- Lay them straight — eyeball or measure
- **Why 4 feet:** that's the natural walk stride for an average horse. Too close, he stops or trips. Too far, he stretches awkwardly.

### Station 2 — Side-Pass Over a Pole
- 1 pole on the ground
- 12 feet long if you have it (longer = easier alignment)
- Approach: walk up parallel to the pole, halt over it (the pole is **under the horse's belly**)
- Cue: leg behind cinch on one side, slight rein support on the other
- 3 sideways steps to clear it
- **Easy failure mode:** horse steps forward or back instead of sideways. Reset and ask again.

### Station 3 — 5-Cone Weave
- 5 cones in a straight line
- **Spacing: 8 feet apart, cone-to-cone**
- Weave through one-handed (Week 11 standard)
- Eyes on the *next* cone, not the one you're passing
- **Why 8 feet:** tight enough to require real steering, wide enough to do at the walk without slamming knees on the cones

### Station 4 — Gate
- **Option A (best):** Use your actual arena gate. Side-pass parallel, unlatch, push through, halt, side-pass back, close, latch.
- **Option B:** Run a rope between two T-posts or two heavy cones. Loop the rope to one post with a slip-knot. Use it as a "gate" — unhook, ride through, rehook.
- **Width:** 10-12 feet between posts. Wide enough to ride through comfortably.
- **Practice both directions** — open left-handed AND right-handed. Trails go both ways.

### Station 5 — Tarp Walk
- 6' × 8' tarp on the ground
- **Weight every corner** with a sandbag, rock, or stake. A flapping tarp is a different obstacle (and a harder one).
- Approach straight, walk over slowly. Look ahead, not down.
- **If your horse refuses:** halt, breathe, ask again. Don't force. Worst case, dismount and lead him over (counts as a win), then remount and try mounted.

### Station 6 — Back Through an L
- Build an L-shape with poles: think a hallway that turns 90°
- **Aisle width: 4 feet minimum** (between the two parallel poles)
- Walk forward into the L, halt, back up through it
- Look over your shoulder while backing — watch where the horse's hind end is going
- Use seat + voice + slight rein. Don't haul.

### Station 7 — Halt, Count to 10, Bow
- Walk to the center after the last obstacle
- Halt
- Sit deep, breathe slow, **count out loud to 10**
- Bow your head (or the horse can lower his)
- Pat the horse. Smile.
- This is the finish line. Train it like it matters — the horse should know when the work is done.

## How to ride the course

**First ride (week 11, day 1):**
- Walk through the whole course slowly. Don't worry about flow.
- Take 10-20 seconds between obstacles to reset.
- Ride two-handed for stations 1, 2, 5, 6. One-handed for 3, 4.
- Film it from outside the arena.

**Second ride (week 11, day 2):**
- Same course, all stations one-handed where possible.
- Try to flow between obstacles without stopping.

**Third ride (week 11, day 3):**
- Walk version, then **jog the easier obstacles** (1 and 3).
- Don't jog the side-pass, gate, tarp, or L-back — those are walk-only obstacles by design.

## How to scale this beyond Week 11

After Week 12, when you want to keep using this course:

| Difficulty | Modification |
|---|---|
| **Easier** | Wider cone spacing (10-12'), shorter weave (3 cones), no tarp, faux gate (rope) |
| **Same** | As drawn |
| **Harder** | Tighter cone spacing (6'), 7 cones, real gate, tarp un-weighted (flaps), L-aisle 3' wide |
| **Trail-realistic** | Add a bridge (4'×8' plywood on 2x4s), a water tray, a wooden log, a slicker hanging from a fence |

## Filming tips

- **Set the tripod outside the arena**, side angle, covering 3-4 stations
- **Reposition halfway through** to catch the second half (or use two phones if you have one to spare)
- **Husband on his own horse?** Have him film with one hand while riding behind you — surprisingly stable, and shows the whole course
- **Save the video** with the date in the filename. You'll watch it again in a year and not believe it.

---

## The point of this course

It's not a test. It's not a competition. It's a structured way to **prove to yourself** that you can ride a horse one-handed through a real-world chain of decisions:

- Step over obstacles → trail confidence
- Side-pass → opening gates, parking parallel
- Weave → steering with body
- Gate → the single most useful trail skill
- Tarp → handling spooky things
- L-back → control of the hind end (also: backing a horse trailer mentally)
- Halt and bow → calm finish

By Week 12, this should feel routine. That's when you know you're ready for the graduation trail ride.
