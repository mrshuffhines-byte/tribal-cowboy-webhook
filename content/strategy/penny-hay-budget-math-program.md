# Penny's Hay Budget — The 4-Week Math Sprint
## Full Program Specification

**Target grades:** 1st through 3rd grade (ages 6–9)
**Duration:** 4 weekly visits, 45 min each, plus 3 between-visit pen-pal letters
**Pony for school visits:** A small calm mini named **Penny** (Penny is the brand-facing name for the math program — substitute whichever pony actually does the visits)
**Big-horse character:** **Abby the Clydesdale** — the kids meet Penny in class but the whole program is about figuring out how to feed Abby. **Abby appears in person ONLY at Visit 4** as the "BIG REVEAL" capstone moment.
**Cost to school:** Free
**Cost to sponsor:** $2,000 per school

> **Program structure note (updated 2026-05-11):** Penny the pony is the classroom character for Visits 1, 2, and 3. The Clydesdale (Abby) appears in person ONLY at Visit 4 — either at the school yard or, ideally, at a field trip to the farm. The whole math curriculum is built around figuring out how to feed Abby. Kids hear about her in every visit and every pen-pal letter, but they don't meet her until the final day. That three-week buildup is what makes the reveal land.

---

## Why this works as a sister product to the Reading Sprint

- Same structural model (4 weeks, weekly visits, between-visit letters, Visit 4 culmination)
- Targets a slightly older grade band (1–3 vs K–2) so a school could host BOTH Sprints in the same year for different classes
- The financial literacy angle is **the perfect sponsor magnet for banks and credit unions** — it sells itself to Mountain West Bank, Idaho Central Credit Union, Washington Trust, and any community lender
- Builds on Mobile Whinnies' financial literacy idea (their mini is named Knox) but makes it real with actual ranch numbers — kids learn from a working business, not a generic worksheet
- The "Pony Market" at Visit 4 mirrors Mobile Whinnies' "Kid Entrepreneur Markets" — a proven, beloved format

---

## Why grades 1–3 (not K)

- Money concepts require number sense beyond counting — kindergarten is too early
- 1st grade: addition/subtraction within 20, intro to coins (1.OA.1, 1.OA.6, 1.MD.3)
- 2nd grade: working with dollars and cents, place value to 100s (2.OA.1, 2.MD.8)
- 3rd grade: multiplication, real-world word problems, multi-step (3.OA.3, 3.NBT.2)
- Kids in this band love big numbers and gross-out facts — "Abby eats 25 pounds of hay a day" is exactly the right level of shocking

A mixed 1st–3rd grade group works fine — the older kids do the multiplication, the younger kids do the counting and addition. Same lesson, different math floors.

## Visit 1 — "Meet Penny" (Week 1)

**Theme:** Hello, I'm a pony with a problem. We have to feed my big friend Abby.

**Pre-visit Monday:** Teacher reads the kids a "Welcome from Penny" letter announcing the visit.

### Run-of-show (45 min)

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival + introduction | Stacie introduces herself + Penny. Brief land acknowledgment. *"Penny is small, but she has a HUGE problem. She has a friend named Abby, and Abby is a Clydesdale, which is the biggest kind of horse there is. And Abby is hungry. Like... really hungry. Penny is asking us for help."* |
| 0:05–0:15 | The Hay Problem | Show a real flake of hay. *"This is one flake of hay. It weighs about 5 pounds. Penny eats one flake a day. Abby eats FIVE flakes a day."* Stack 5 flakes (or use boxes labeled as flakes). Kids gasp. |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Counting hay** | Activity: kids work in small groups to figure out how much hay each pony eats per week. Penny: 1 flake × 7 days = 7 flakes/week. Abby: 5 flakes × 7 days = 35 flakes/week. Then per month (multiply by 4). The numbers get big fast. |
| 0:30–0:40 | Read-aloud + sponsor moment | Read a short book about a working ranch or financial smarts (suggestions in materials list). Or do a "Penny says" Q&A about ranch life. |
| 0:40–0:45 | Goodbye + Hay Budget Booklet | Hand out the **Hay Budget Booklet** (8 pages, kids fill out across 4 weeks). Tell them Penny will write them a letter. |

### Materials
- Penny (small, calm, school-trained mini or pony)
- Halter, lead, mat
- Real hay flakes (1 flake) OR labeled cardboard boxes the same size
- Sight word / vocab cards: count, add, total, day, week
- Hay Budget Booklets, one per kid (template at bottom)
- Cleanup kit
- Insurance certificate + waivers
- Phone for setup/teacher photos

## Letter 1 — From Penny, Between Visit 1 and Visit 2

> Hi friends!
>
> Thank you SO much for helping me with the hay problem. I went home and told Abby that you figured out she eats **35 flakes of hay every week**. Abby blushed. Then she ate another flake.
>
> Here is the math you helped me with:
>
> **1 flake = 5 pounds**
> **Abby eats 5 flakes a day = 25 pounds**
> **In a week, Abby eats 25 × 7 = 175 pounds of hay**
>
> One hundred and seventy-five pounds! That is more than your teacher weighs!
>
> But hay isn't the only thing Abby needs. This week I want you to think about what else a horse might cost. Here are this week's words:
>
> **dollar, cost, expense, buy, save**
>
> Try this with your grown-up tonight. Ask them: *"What is one thing in our house that costs more than ten dollars?"* Bring your answer back to class on Wednesday.
>
> Next week we are going to look at REAL prices for horse care. Spoiler — it is a lot. We need to figure out how much Stacie spends every week to keep Abby and me happy.
>
> Practice your math!
>
> Love,
> **Penny 🐴**
> *(hoof print here)*

## Visit 2 — "The Real Cost of a Horse" (Week 2)

**Theme:** Horses cost money. Lots of it. Let's add it up.

### Run-of-show (45 min)

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Welcome + recap | "Did anyone find something at home that costs more than ten dollars?" Quick share. |
| 0:05–0:15 | The Real Numbers | Stacie introduces 5 cost cards with real (or close to real) ranch numbers: **Hay: $15/week** • **Grain: $10/week** • **Vet visit: $80 every 6 months** • **Hoof trimming: $50 every 8 weeks** • **Treats: $5/week**. Kids react. |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Adding up the week** | Group activity: figure out how much Stacie spends per week to keep Abby happy. Kids add: $15 (hay) + $10 (grain) + $5 (treats) = $30/week (basic). With pro-rated vet and hoof: roughly $40/week. Older kids do the multi-step math; younger kids do the addition. |
| 0:30–0:40 | Read-aloud or "Penny Q&A" | Choose a short money-themed read-aloud (suggestions below) or do a structured Q&A where kids ask Penny questions about ranch life and Stacie answers. |
| 0:40–0:45 | Goodbye + tease | "Next week, YOU get to make a budget. You get fake money. You get to choose what to buy for the pony. But you don't have enough for everything. Get ready." |

### Materials
- Same as Visit 1
- 5 large laminated "Cost Cards" with prices and pictures
- New vocab cards: dollar, cost, expense, buy, save
- New read-aloud book (see suggestions)
- Hay Budget Booklets (kids continue filling them in)

## Letter 2 — From Penny, Between Visit 2 and Visit 3

> Hi class!
>
> Last week was BRAIN DAY. You all did so much math. Penny brain is tired. Penny brain needs a nap.
>
> But before I nap, I have a CHALLENGE for you. Next week is the BIG one. You are going to be ranch managers.
>
> Here is what is going to happen. You are each going to get **fifty fake dollars**. That is your weekly budget for taking care of Penny and Abby. You will have to choose what to buy.
>
> But — and this is the tricky part — **you cannot buy everything**. Fifty dollars is not enough for hay AND grain AND a vet visit AND treats AND new shoes for Abby's giant feet (yes, horses wear shoes, can you believe it?).
>
> So you are going to have to make CHOICES. Some things are needs (hay — Abby will literally cry without it). Some things are wants (treats — Abby loves them but can survive a week).
>
> This week's words:
>
> **budget, choice, need, want, plan**
>
> Try this at home. Look in your kitchen. Find one thing that is a NEED (you would be hungry without it) and one thing that is a WANT (you would be sad without it but you would be okay).
>
> Bring your ideas to class. We are going to need them.
>
> Get ready, ranch managers.
>
> Love,
> **Penny 🐴**
> *(hoof print here)*
>
> P.S. Don't tell Abby this is happening. She will get nervous about the treat budget.

## Visit 3 — "Ranch Manager Day" (Week 3)

**Theme:** Real-life budgeting with constraints. The kids are in charge.

This is the magical visit for Math Sprint — same way Visit 3 is magical for Reading. Kids get to be in charge. Older kids especially love it because it feels grown-up.

### Run-of-show (45 min)

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Welcome + handout | Each kid gets an envelope of $50 in fake money (5 ten-dollar bills, prop money) |
| 0:05–0:15 | The Ranch Store | Stations set up around the room with the cost cards: Hay ($15), Grain ($10), Vet ($20), Hoof care ($10), Treats ($5), New saddle pad ($25). Kids walk around, decide what to "buy" |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Make a budget** | Each kid sits with their booklet and writes down their plan. Teacher and Stacie circulate, helping kids who are stuck. *"You can't afford the saddle pad AND the vet visit AND grain. What do you cut? Why?"* |
| 0:30–0:40 | Share-out | Volunteers share what they bought and why. Stacie (as Penny) reacts: *"Aww, you didn't get any treats? That's okay, we can save up for next week."* Kids learn there is no wrong answer if they explain their reasoning. |
| 0:40–0:45 | Goodbye + BIG tease | "Next week is our last visit. And we have a HUGE surprise. Abby herself is going to come to school. AND we are going to set up a Pony Market where you sell things you make. Get ready." |

### Materials
- $50 in fake money per kid (10s, 5s, 1s — reusable)
- 6 cost station cards
- Hay Budget Booklets (kids work directly in them)
- Pencils
- All previous materials

### Pro tip
This visit can run long. Some kids will want to spend 20 minutes deliberating. Others will spend it all on treats in 30 seconds. Both are fine — the lesson lands either way.

## Letter 3 — From Penny, Between Visit 3 and Visit 4

> Dear ranch managers,
>
> I am so proud I might gallop in circles. Last week you all made REAL BUDGETS for me and Abby. Some of you bought treats. Some of you bought the vet visit. Some of you saved your money. There were no wrong answers.
>
> But Stacie said something important. She said in real life, money does not just appear. Someone has to **earn** it.
>
> So next week, we are going to change the game. Instead of just spending money, you are going to EARN it.
>
> Here is what is going to happen. We are setting up the **Pony Market**. Each kid will make ONE thing — a drawing, a craft, a small sign, a poem, a card — and you will "sell" it at the market. The other kids in class are your customers. You will use your fake money from last week to buy from each other. The kid who earns the most pony bucks at the end gets a special prize from me.
>
> AND — drum roll — **Abby is coming to school**. I have warned her she is too big to come inside. So we will all go outside. She is excited. She is also worried about how long the bus ride is. (I told her it's just a few miles. She did not believe me.)
>
> This week's words:
>
> **earn, market, profit, work, share**
>
> One last thing. I want to thank you. Four weeks ago I came to your class and you didn't know how much hay I eat. Now you know how to budget for a whole ranch. You are not the same kids I met in Visit 1. You grew up.
>
> See you Wednesday for the BIG day.
>
> Love forever,
> **Penny 🐴**
> *(hoof print here)*
>
> P.S. Bring something simple to "sell" at the Pony Market — a drawing, a card, a craft. It can be tiny. It just needs a price.

## Visit 4 — "The Pony Market + Meet Abby" (Week 4)

Same two-option structure as the Reading Sprint.

### Option A — Field trip to the farm (preferred)

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Class arrives, greet at gate | Walking tour to the barn |
| 15 min | Meet Abby in person | Kids see the actual Clydesdale. Stacie shows them the pile of hay Abby eats in a day. They calculate it's "the math we did in Week 1." |
| 20 min | **The Pony Market** | Set up at the farm with a simple table. Kids "sell" their crafts to each other using their fake money. Stacie can also have a "Penny's Lemonade Stand" or "Penny's Treat Bar" where kids can spend earnings. They learn to count change. |
| 15 min | Wagon ride | Abby pulls the wagon. Stacie quietly mentions that Abby's wagon ride costs $200 in real life — not because of the wagon, because of feeding her. Kids gasp. |
| 10 min | Closing + math celebration | Quick review of all 20 math words. Kids who can answer 3 word problems correctly get the "Ranch Manager" stamp on their booklet. |
| 10 min | Photo session + take-homes | Same as Reading Sprint — Reader Certificate equivalent is the **Ranch Manager Certificate**, photo magnet, take-home one-pager |

### Option B — Final school visit

If field trip not approved:

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival + welcome | Outdoors, weather permitting |
| 0:05–0:15 | Meet Abby outside | Same impact — kids see scale, real animal |
| 0:15–0:35 | **Pony Market in the parking lot or on the grass** | Same setup as Option A. Kids sell to each other, count change, learn to make profit |
| 0:35–0:40 | Math celebration + word review | All 20 math words |
| 0:40–0:45 | Goodbye + take-homes | Same gifts as Option A |

### Take-home gifts
- **Hay Budget Booklet** stamped with the Ranch Manager star
- **Ranch Manager Certificate** signed by Penny's hoof-print: "I budgeted, I bought, I sold, I earned. I am a Ranch Manager."
- Photo magnet of Penny + Abby (no kid faces if pre-printed)
- Take-home one-pager for parents (template below)
