## What's Different About the Pre-K Sprint

Pre-K kids (3–5) don't need sight words yet. They need:

- **Vocabulary** — they're learning words as fast as their brains can build them
- **Print awareness** — books have a front and a back, words go left to right, pictures tell stories
- **Listening comprehension** — can they follow a story?
- **Pre-phonological awareness** — rhyming, syllables, beginning sounds
- **Letter recognition** — the very first letter of their name
- **Pre-writing** — drawing, mark-making
- **Self-regulation** — a tool for big feelings (Pony Breath)

This Sprint hits all seven. Without ever asking a 4-year-old to decode a CVC word.

**Idaho alignment:** Idaho Early Learning eGuidelines, Communication-Language-Literacy domain. The Pre-K curriculum doesn't use formal ELA standards — but every activity below maps to the state's pre-K literacy markers.

## The Signature Mechanic — A Custom Book Delivered by the Pony

At **Visit 2**, the pony walks into the classroom with a leather mailbag full of books. She delivers one book to each kid. The book is titled *The Pony Goes to School*, written by Stacie, illustrated with real photos of the actual pony.

Every kid takes their book home that day. The book stays on their shelf. Parents read it. The Tribal Cowboy logo and sponsor logo live in 15+ households for the rest of the kid's childhood.

This is the program's biggest single brand asset. See the full draft text and production guide below.

## The Pony Wears a Costume Every Visit

Pre-K kids love costumes. They make every visit feel like a special event. They photograph beautifully. They give the pony a "character" the kids remember.

**Important:** Test every costume element at home for at least 3 sessions BEFORE the school visit. Some ponies tolerate mailbags but flinch at flower garlands. Some are fine with bandanas but hate hats. Know your pony.

| Visit | Costume | What it signals | Production cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visit 1** | **Pony Express Rider** — leather/canvas mailbag, red bandana around neck | "I have a job. I deliver mail." | ~$30 (mailbag DIY or Etsy) + $5 bandana |
| **Visit 2** | **Cowboy Pony** — different colored bandana (blue), Western saddle blanket, mailbag returns (loaded with books!) | "I came from the ranch." | $15 saddle blanket + bandana |
| **Visit 3** | **Friend Pony** — silk flower garland clipped to halter, small stuffed animal "friend" attached to saddlebag | "Today is about friends." | $5 silk garland (DIY) + thrift-store stuffed animal |
| **Visit 4** | **Celebration Pony** — large floral wreath on halter, ribbon bow at the saddle, photo-ready saddle pad | "It's the big day!" | $15 flower wreath + ribbon |

**For Abby at Visit 4:** Simple bow on her halter, optional small garland. She's huge — less is more. The visual impact comes from her size, not the dress-up.

**Costume safety rules:**
- Nothing that restricts the pony's vision
- Nothing that restricts movement or breathing
- Nothing with small parts a kid could pull off and choke on
- All elements quick on/off (under 3 min)
- Practice every new element at home before the visit
- The pony's halter is always functional — costume goes OVER, never replaces it

# The Visual Schedule (used at every visit)

A small felt board with 6 laminated picture cards. Move a clip down the board as you go.

[👋 Hello] → [🐴 Pony Body] → [🫁 Pony Breath] → [📖 Story] → [✋ Touch] → [👋 Goodbye]

Kids check the board between activities. Many will walk up to look mid-visit. Don't redirect.

---

# The Pony Breath Ritual

Stacie says: *"Pony Breath."*

Kids put a hand on their belly, look at the pony, match her breath. One slow breath in. One slow breath out. Three rounds.

Used at least twice per visit. **Teach explicitly at Visit 1.** By Visit 4, kids do it on their own.

# Visit 1 — "Meet the Pony Express Rider"

**Theme:** I am a pony. I have a job. We can be gentle with each other.
**Costume:** Pony Express Rider (mailbag + red bandana)
**Literacy focus:** Vocabulary + print awareness + listening comprehension

## Pre-Visit (Monday before Visit 1)

The teacher reads the class a short Welcome Letter from the pony.

> Hi friends!
>
> My name is a secret until Wednesday — that's when I'm coming to your school to meet you!
>
> I am a pony. My owner is Stacie. We live at a place called Tribal Cowboy in Athol, Idaho.
>
> When I come, I will wear my mail bag because I have a job. I am a Pony Express pony. I deliver letters.
>
> See you soon!
>
> — Your pony friend 🐴

## Run-of-Show — 25 minutes

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:03** | Hello + visual schedule. Stacie introduces herself in one sentence. Wave hello to the pony as a group. *Notice the pony's mailbag — "She has a job today."* |
| **0:03–0:09** | **Pony body parts.** Point at parts and name slowly: **hoof, mane, tail, eye.** Kids touch their own body at each part. Count together: 4 hooves, 2 eyes, 1 tail. |
| **0:09–0:11** | **Pony Breath #1 — first teach.** Hand on belly. Slow in, slow out. Three rounds. |
| **0:11–0:18** | **Read-aloud:** *Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?* by Bill Martin Jr. **— substitute "pony" into the rhyme.** Kids on hay-bale spots or yoga squares, semicircle. Repetitive, predictable, perfect for Pre-K. |
| **0:18–0:22** | **Sensory touch.** One kid at a time, 20 sec touch (mane, then shoulder). Vocabulary: **soft.** Other kids on their spot markers count the pony's hooves or pet a hay sample. |
| **0:22–0:24** | **Pony Breath #2** + Pony Pals Sticker Book starter page. Each kid puts the first sticker on their book. |
| **0:24–0:25** | **Wave goodbye.** *"Watch your mailbox — a letter is coming Monday."* |

## Literacy Skills Hit

- **Vocabulary:** hoof, mane, tail, eye, soft (5 new words taught + practiced)
- **Print awareness:** Stacie holds the book up showing the cover, points at the title, turns pages left to right
- **Listening comprehension:** Kids follow the rhyme of *Brown Bear*
- **Phonological awareness (intro):** The rhyming pattern of *Brown Bear* exposes kids to rhyme
- **Self-regulation:** Pony Breath introduced

# Visit 2 — "The Mail Arrives" 🐴📬

**Theme:** The pony brings books. Words have parts you can count.
**Costume:** Cowboy Pony (blue bandana + Western saddle blanket + mailbag full of books)
**Literacy focus:** Phonological awareness (syllable counting) + colors + the book as a physical object

**This is the visit kids will remember for years.** The pony walks in with a mailbag. The bag is full of books. Every kid gets their own copy of *The Pony Goes to School* — delivered by the pony herself.

## Run-of-Show — 25 minutes

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:03** | Hello + visual schedule. *"Today the pony brought you something. Can you see her mailbag? It looks full."* |
| **0:03–0:05** | **Pony Breath.** Already familiar. Kids do it more independently. |
| **0:05–0:13** | **🐴 THE BOOK DELIVERY MOMENT.** Stacie pulls books out of the pony's mailbag one at a time, calls each kid's name, kid walks up to the pony, takes their book directly from the pony's saddlebag. Photo opportunity. Each kid says *"Thank you, pony!"* and walks back to their spot. |
| **0:13–0:19** | **Read-aloud: *The Pony Goes to School*** — kids follow along in their own copy. Stacie reads one page at a time, kids hold their own books and turn the pages with her. **First time a Pre-K kid "reads" a book.** |
| **0:19–0:22** | **🐴 Syllable Paws.** Stacie names an animal. Class says it. *"How many beats? Let's ask the pony."* Pony paws — *clop, clop* — kids count out loud. Words: **goat** (1), **pon-y** (2), **but-ter-fly** (3). Then kids stomp the syllables of their own name. |
| **0:22–0:24** | **Pony Breath** + new sticker for the Pony Pals book. |
| **0:24–0:25** | Goodbye. *"Take your book home. Read it with your grown-up. I'll write to you Monday."* |

## Literacy Skills Hit

- **Vocabulary:** brown, white, hay, friend, gentle (5 new words)
- **Print awareness:** Each kid holds their own book. Turns the pages. Points at the words.
- **Phonological awareness:** Syllable counting (1, 2, 3 beats) with the pony's hoof
- **Pre-reading:** Kids follow along while Stacie reads — first taste of reading a real book on their own
- **Color recognition (embedded):** brown like the pony's mane, white like her star

# Visit 3 — "Pony's Friends"

**Theme:** The pony has friends. So do we. Friends are kind.
**Costume:** Friend Pony (silk flower garland + stuffed animal "friend" on saddlebag)
**Literacy focus:** Rhyming + story sequencing + social-emotional vocabulary

## Run-of-Show — 25 minutes

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:03** | Hello + visual schedule. Acknowledge the pony's flower garland. Where do flowers grow? |
| **0:03–0:05** | **Pony Breath.** |
| **0:05–0:11** | **Pony's friends.** Show 3–4 photos of the real friends at the ranch — goat, cat, another pony. Vocabulary: **share, kind, help, listen, breathe.** Kids act out each word with their body. |
| **0:11–0:15** | **🐴 The Pony Picks.** Stacie holds up 3 friend photos. The pony nose-touches one. Kids cheer. *"The pony wants to tell you about her goat friend today."* Stacie shares one fun fact about that friend. Repeat 1–2 more rounds. |
| **0:15–0:18** | **Rhyme time.** "What rhymes with **pony**?" Kids guess — bony, only, lonely. Then: "What rhymes with **hay**?" — day, play, say. Pony stomps once for each rhyme that fits. |
| **0:18–0:22** | **Read-aloud:** *We're Going on a Bear Hunt* by Michael Rosen — rhyming, predictable, action-based. Kids do the motions when each obstacle appears (swishy grass, splashy river, squelchy mud). Pre-K loves this. |
| **0:22–0:24** | **Pony Breath** + draw your best friend in the Pony Pals book. |
| **0:24–0:25** | Goodbye. *"Next week is our LAST visit. I'm bringing my BIG friend Abby."* |

## Literacy Skills Hit

- **Vocabulary:** share, kind, help, listen, breathe (5 new words)
- **Phonological awareness:** Rhyming — the second pillar of early literacy. Pre-K kids who can rhyme by age 5 are more likely to read on time.
- **Story sequencing:** *Bear Hunt* repetitive structure teaches story arc
- **Social-emotional learning:** Friendship vocabulary acted out

## Why this book works for Pre-K

| Feature | Why it's right for ages 3–5 |
|---|---|
| **~50 words total** | Pre-K kids can handle 4–10 words per page max |
| **Repetitive structure** ("The pony has...", "She walks...") | Repetition is a core Pre-K reading mechanic — they predict the next page |
| **One clear photo per page** | Pre-K kids "read" pictures before words |
| **Single sentence per page** | They learn that one line = one idea |
| **Real photos of the actual pony** | The pony they just met is in the book — kids lose their minds |
| **The story matches their experience** | They literally lived this story at Visit 2 |
| **Predictable arc** | Ranch → mailbag → walk → school → kids → home |

## Production Guide — How to Make the Book

### Step 1 — Take the photos (1 weekend at the ranch)

Take **30+ high-res photos** of the pony in different positions and settings:
- The pony in the pasture, morning light
- The pony with her mailbag attached (use the Visit 1 costume)
- Mailbag open showing letters or papers inside
- The pony walking through trees (wooded trail behind the ranch)
- The pony on an open dirt trail
- The pony at a fence or gate
- The pony with hay
- The pony walking away (sunset / wide angle)
- The pony at the ranch sign

**No kid faces in the book.** Hands and arms only when needed. Keeps photo releases simple.

### Step 2 — Lay out the book in Canva (1 weekend)

- Canva Pro account: $13/month (cancel after layout is done)
- Template: "Children's Picture Book" — 5.5×8.5 size, 12 pages
- One photo per spread, one line of text per page
- Use a simple, friendly font: Caveat, Fredoka, or Quicksand (all free in Canva)
- Add the Pony Express stamp graphic to the title page
- Add Tribal Cowboy logo to the back cover
- Add space for the sponsor logo (variable per Sprint)

### Step 3 — Print (1 week turnaround)

**Option 1 — Local print shop** (Office Depot, FedEx, or independent):
- Saddle-stitched booklet, full color, 12 pages
- 5.5×8.5 size (letter folded in half)
- Quote at quantity 20, 50, 100
- **Expected cost:** $1.50–$3 per book at 20 quantity
- **Time:** 3–5 business days

**Option 2 — Amazon KDP** (print-on-demand):
- Free to set up, $0 upfront
- Per-book cost: $4–$8 for a 24-page softcover (note: KDP minimum is 24 pages — pad with sponsor pages + author bio + activity pages)
- Time: 4–6 weeks for initial setup, then 1 week reorder
- **Pro:** Real ISBN, real book on Amazon, professional appearance
- **Con:** Slower, more expensive per copy

**Recommendation:** Start with Option 1 (local print shop) for the first 2–3 Sprints. Once the format is proven, graduate to Option 2 for the polished version.

### Step 4 — Stock the mailbag

15 books per Sprint, plus 3 extras (kids who join late, demo copies, sponsor copy). Pony's mailbag holds all 15.

# Pony Welfare Notes (Pre-K Specific)

Pre-K classes are LOUDER than K-2 classes. Younger kids have less impulse control. The pony is going to be in a room where 15 kids might shriek with delight at once. Pick accordingly:

- Use the calmest pony in your herd, period
- Limit Pre-K visits to 3 per week (not 4–5) — the noise is harder on the pony than K-2 chaos
- ALWAYS have an adult chaperone between the kids and the pony — never let a Pre-K kid approach unsupervised
- Bring water for the pony and a hay net to chew on during the visit
- Required between visits: 24+ hours recovery time

**Costume welfare:**
- Test every costume element at home for 3+ sessions before the visit
- If the pony flinches at the flower garland, skip the garland — don't fight it
- Costume goes OVER the functional halter, never replaces it
- Costume comes off in the parking lot before loading her back into the trailer — she doesn't ride home in costume

# Final Note

The Pre-K Sprint works because it does five things at once:

1. **Kids build vocabulary** — 20 new words, all anchored to a real animal they touched
2. **Kids build pre-reading skills** — print awareness, rhyming, syllables, first letters
3. **Kids regulate emotions** — Pony Breath becomes theirs forever
4. **Kids own a book** — *The Pony Goes to School* lives on their shelf with the sponsor's logo on the back cover
5. **The pony has a job** — costumes give her a character every visit, and the kids see her as a working animal with real work to do

The signature moves at Pre-K specifically:
- **The book delivery at Visit 2** — the pony walks in with books for every kid. This is the moment teachers will call other teachers about.
- **The costume rotation** — kids count down the days because they want to see what the pony is wearing this week
- **The custom book** — every kid takes home a real book about the real pony they just met, with the sponsor's name on the back

Build those three things well and the Pre-K Sprint sells itself.
