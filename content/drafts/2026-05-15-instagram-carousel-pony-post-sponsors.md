## BRIEF FOR DESIGNER OR STACIE

Visual direction overall: Warm and business-credible. Not a charity ask. Not a marketing deck. The tone is: "here is a real thing we do, here is what it means for you." Use gold, cream, and deep green. Include the sponsor logo placement mockup on Slide 4 — this is the most important visual in the carousel. If you have a mock activity book cover with a placeholder logo (e.g., "Your Logo Here" in a branded box), build it. That one image does more selling than three slides of copy.

## SLIDES

---

### SLIDE 1 — COVER / HOOK

**Headline text (large, bold):**
Your name on the books 18 kids made themselves.

**Subhead (smaller):**
The Pony Post — a four-week kindergarten literacy program.
Sponsored by one North Idaho business per Sprint.

**Visual direction:**
A photo of a child's hands holding open an activity book — showing stamps, drawings, their name written in big kid letters. The Tribal Cowboy logo (or a placeholder sponsor logo box on the cover) visible in the corner. Warm lighting. Real, not staged. If this photo doesn't exist yet, use a flat-lay of a decorated activity book with crayons and a stamped envelope nearby — show the kid-made quality of what this is.

**Note:** "Books 18 kids made themselves" is doing the work that "brand exposure" cannot do for this audience. It is specific, visual, and immediately different from any sponsorship ask a local business gets from a 5K organizer or a chamber of commerce lunch.

---

### SLIDE 2 — WHAT THE PROGRAM IS

**Headline:**
A pony visits the same classroom four weeks in a row.

**Body text:**
Each week has a different theme and story. Between visits, every kid gets a stamped envelope — a letter from the pony with a clue about next week and a new word to look for.

Real letters. Real stamps. Real pony.

Over four weeks, each child builds their own Pony Post Adventure Book — they decorate it, write in it, stamp it, and take it home when the Sprint ends.

**Visual direction:**
Simple illustrated mail route or timeline — four numbered stops, connected by a dotted line that looks like a mail path. Warm gold and teal. This can live in Canva. It does not need to be photographed.

---

### SLIDE 3 — WHAT "SPONSORED BY" ACTUALLY MEANS

**Headline:**
One business. One classroom. One Sprint.

**Body text:**
The cost to the school is zero. A single local business sponsor covers one Sprint — four visits, all materials, all letters, Stacie's time and travel, the pony's working day.

One Sprint: $1,750.

That covers 18 kids, four weeks of visits, 18 activity books, 72 Pony Post letters (4 per child), and all themed take-homes. Every piece that goes home has your name on it.

**Visual direction:**
Clean breakdown on a cream background. The $1,750 number should not hide — put it on the slide clearly. Business owners respect transparency. Below the number, a simple bulleted breakdown of what it covers. Use a thin gold rule or divider between the price and the list. Small Tribal Cowboy logo bottom right.

---

### SLIDE 4 — WHERE YOUR NAME ACTUALLY LIVES

**Headline:**
Your logo. 18 homes. Four weeks.

**Body text:**
Every item that leaves the classroom has your name on it:

- The cover of every activity book each child built
- Every Pony Post letter sent home (4 per child — 72 total)
- The parent one-pager sent home at the end of the Sprint
- The photo print every family gets of their child with the pony

The activity book is the one that matters. A Pre-K kid who built something themselves does not put it on a shelf. It goes on the refrigerator door.

**Visual direction:**
This is the most important slide in the carousel. Show a mockup: an activity book cover with "Sponsored by [Your Logo Here]" placed where the sponsor block would live. Clean, not cluttered. Next to or below it, a small Pony Post letter with the sponsor name visible in the header. If you have a real sponsor from a pilot Sprint, use their actual logo with permission and add a testimonial pull quote. If not, use a clean placeholder mockup.

### SLIDE 5 — THE CONVERSATION AT DINNER

**Headline:**
The program their child cannot stop talking about.

**Body text:**
A pony walked into their kindergarten classroom.
It had a costume.
It sent them a letter.
They got to stamp their own book.

This is what families talk about at dinner for weeks. Your name is on every piece they bring home from that conversation.

No ad does that.

**Visual direction:**
A candid photo — a child showing a parent or sibling the activity book, or pointing at a letter. If no photo exists, use a close-up of a child's excited face at a pony visit. Warm, real. A single line of overlay text at the bottom: "Your name on the book. Every night it comes off the fridge." (optional — use only if the photo can carry the text without crowding.)

**Note:** This slide is intentionally brief. The image does the work. Do not over-explain. The business owner reading this already knows what it means.

- **Best days:** Thursday or Friday

- **Boost:** Yes — $25–35 targeted at North Idaho and Eastern Washington. Interests: small business, community events, local business ownership. Age 30–60. ## NOTES

- The $1,750 figure is on Slide 3 and in the caption. Do not soften or remove it. Business owners who are going to say yes need to know the number before they DM. Business owners who are going to say no will say no faster, which saves everyone time.
- "Tax-deductible as a community giving expense — check with your accountant on the specifics" is intentional hedging. Tribal Cowboy should not be giving tax advice. The sentence acknowledges it without claiming it as a guarantee.
- Indigenous identity appears once, in the sign-off line, as a factual identification — not in the pitch, not as a value-add hook.
- If Stacie has a real sponsor from a pilot Sprint, Slide 4 and the caption can be updated to reference them by name (with permission). A real example is stronger than a mockup.
- This goes through the Brand Voice agent before it posts.
