# The Pony Post — Insurance Memo
## Coverage Questions for Tribal Cowboy LLC's Equine Liability Carrier
### To be completed BEFORE the first Pony Post Sprint is scheduled

**Status:** Internal working document — call notes for Stacie's insurance carrier
**Why this exists:** The Council flagged that any Idaho school district's risk manager will ask whether Stacie's equine activity liability policy covers school-site visits with a live animal. Without documentation in hand, the program does not get past the principal's desk. This memo is the script for that call.

---

## Step 1 — Call your current insurance carrier

Before scheduling the call, gather the following:
- Your existing policy number and effective dates
- Coverage limits on your current general liability policy (per occurrence + aggregate)
- The first three school names you intend to pitch (helps the underwriter scope)

Then ask the carrier these questions, in this order. Take notes. Get answers in writing if possible (request a follow-up email after the call).

### Question 1 — Off-site coverage
> "Does my current general liability policy cover **off-site school visits with a miniature pony**, where I am bringing the animal onto a school campus during normal school hours?"

**Why this matters:** Many equine policies cover the home property only. Off-site events often require a rider or a separate "premises liability for visiting animals" endorsement.

**If the answer is YES:** Move to Question 2.
**If the answer is NO:** Ask immediately, *"What rider or endorsement would I need to add this coverage, and what is the cost?"*

---

### Question 2 — Coverage limits required by school districts
> "Is my current coverage limit at least **$1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate**? Most public school districts in Idaho require this minimum to grant a Certificate of Insurance for an outside vendor."

**Why this matters:** $1M/$2M is the standard floor most public districts require. Some larger districts (Coeur d'Alene, Post Falls, Lakeland) may require $2M/$4M. Know your number before pitching.

**If the answer is YES at $1M/$2M:** Confirm and document.
**If the answer is NO:** Ask, *"What would it cost to raise the limits to $1M/$2M? To $2M/$4M?"*

---

### Question 3 — Certificate of Insurance for school districts
> "Can you issue a **Certificate of Insurance (COI) naming a school district as Additionally Insured** for a one-day or four-week program? How long does it take to generate, and is there a per-COI fee?"

**Why this matters:** The school will require a COI before Stacie sets foot on campus. Knowing the turnaround time matters — if the carrier needs 10 business days, that affects scheduling.

**Document:** Turnaround time. Per-COI fee (usually $0–$50). Whether they can name the school district AND the individual school AND the Sponsor entity, all on the same COI.

---

### Question 4 — Coverage for child contact with the animal
> "The program involves **young children (ages 3–6) touching the pony's mane and shoulder under direct adult supervision**. Are bites, kicks, or accidental injury during contact covered under my current policy, or is this excluded?"

**Why this matters:** Some policies have an "animal contact" exclusion that voids coverage the moment a guest touches the animal. This is the question that determines whether you can run the program at all.

**If the answer reveals an exclusion:** Ask, *"Is there a rider or specialty policy for animal-assisted programs that explicitly covers supervised guest contact? Can you quote it?"*

---

### Question 5 — Idaho Code §6-1801 (Equine Activities Immunity Act)
> "Does my policy interact with **Idaho Code §6-1801, the Equine Activities Immunity Act**? Specifically, if I post the required statutory warning sign and have signed waivers, does that affect my coverage requirements or premium?"

**Why this matters:** Idaho's Equine Activities Immunity Act protects equine professionals from liability for inherent risks of equine activities — but only when the statutory warning is posted and waivers are properly executed. This is a real legal protection layered on top of insurance, not a replacement for it.

**Document:** Whether the carrier acknowledges the statute. Whether posting the warning + signed waivers reduces premium or otherwise affects coverage.

---

### Question 6 — Transportation
> "Is **transport of the pony in my horse trailer to and from a school site** covered, including any liability arising from a transport-related incident on public roads?"

**Why this matters:** General liability and equine policies sometimes do not cover commercial-use transport. Auto/trailer liability may be a separate policy.

**If transport is excluded:** Ask, *"Do I need commercial auto coverage on my truck and trailer for these school visits, or does my personal auto policy suffice?"*

---

### Question 7 — Cost of full coverage
After all the above is answered, ask:
> "Based on what we've discussed, what is the **annual premium** for a policy that covers off-site school visits, $1M/$2M minimum limits, child contact with the animal, and transport, with the ability to issue COIs naming school districts as Additionally Insured?"

**Document the number.** Compare against current premium. Determine the per-Sprint insurance allocation:

> Annual premium ÷ projected annual Sprints = per-Sprint insurance cost

This is the number that goes into the program's true cost basis.

## Reference — Idaho Equine Activities Immunity Act (excerpt)

**Idaho Code §6-1801 et seq.** provides that "an equine activity sponsor [...] shall not be liable for an injury to or the death of a participant resulting from the inherent risks of equine activities." The statute requires:

1. Posting of the statutory warning sign in clearly visible places where equine activities are conducted
2. Inclusion of the statutory warning language in any written contract for equine activities

**The required warning language** (verbatim from §6-1805):

> *"WARNING: Under Idaho law, an equine activity sponsor or equine professional is not liable for an injury to or the death of a participant in equine activities resulting from the inherent risks of equine activities, pursuant to section 6-1801, Idaho Code."*

This warning must appear on every Tribal Cowboy waiver, every sponsor agreement, every school visit consent form. Print it on a small placard to bring to school visits.
