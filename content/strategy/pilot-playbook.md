# Pilot Playbook — Tribal Cowboy Education Program

**Date:** 2026-05-11
**Goal:** Run 2 pilot school visits in the next 30 days. Use the results to lock down corporate sponsors, grant applications, and a real program rollout.
**Read this with:** `mobile-whinnies-emulation-strategy.md` (the strategy doc)

---

## What "pilot" means here

**Updated 2026-05-11 — Sprint + Heritage Visit model** (see `podcast-script-program-design.md` for full reasoning)

A pilot is **two products tested at two schools** that gives you:
1. **Pilot A — One 4-Week Reading Sprint** at a homeschool co-op (4 weekly visits, same class)
2. **Pilot B — One Heritage Visit** at a private school or library (single 60-min visit)

This gives you:
- Photos of the pony in a classroom setting — wide shots, teacher with pony, setup shots (no kid faces required, see Section 11)
- A teacher quote from each pilot ("This was the best thing my class has done all year")
- A parent quote (collected through the take-home one-pager opt-in)
- Anonymous learning outcome data (sight word recognition, kids' favorite word, what they remembered)
- A short highlight reel for sponsors and Instagram
- Real evidence the **Sprint model works** AND real evidence the **single-visit model works** — you'll know which one to lead with for each future school

That's it. You're not selling anything yet. You're **building evidence on two distinct products.**

## Week 1 — Pre-pilot setup (do these in order)

### Day 1 (Monday) — Insurance & legal

1. **Call your current Tribal Cowboy LLC insurer.** Ask them three questions, exactly:
   - "Does my current general liability policy cover off-site school visits with a miniature pony?"
   - "Is the coverage limit at least $1M / $2M aggregate?" (Most schools require this minimum to let you on campus)
   - "Can you issue a Certificate of Insurance naming a school district as additionally insured for a one-day visit?"

2. **If the answer to any is no**, get a rider added or call **Markel Insurance** or **Allen Financial Equine Group** — both write equine event coverage for one-off visits. Budget: $50–$150 for a one-day rider.

3. **Print and save two waivers** (you'll customize these in Day 2):
   - Liability Waiver — Parent/Guardian (for kids under 18)
   - Photo Release — Parent/Guardian
   Steal the layout from Mobile Whinnies' site for structure, then have a North Idaho attorney friend or a $150 LegalZoom review pass over it.

**Idaho legal note:** Idaho Code §6-1801 (Equine Activities Immunity Act) protects equine professionals from liability for inherent risks. Even with that, the waiver + insurance combo is non-negotiable.

### Day 2 (Tuesday) — Build the waiver + photo release packets

- **One-page liability waiver** in plain English: who you are, what the visit involves, that horses are animals and can be unpredictable, parent consents, signature line
- **One-page photo release**: parent checks ONE of three boxes:
  - ☐ Yes, you can use my child's photo in marketing/social media
  - ☐ Yes, you can use my child's photo only on the school's internal materials
  - ☐ No, please do not photograph my child
- Send both to the school **a week before** the visit so teachers can collect signatures during morning sign-in time

### Day 3 (Wednesday) — Design the branded photo overlay

In Canva or your existing design tool, build a single PNG overlay template:

```
[Top right corner]: Tribal Cowboy logo + "Saddle Up & Read"
[Bottom left]: "{SCHOOL NAME} — {DATE}"
[Bottom right]: tribalcowboy.com
[Subtle watermark across the corner]
```

Save this as your master overlay template. For each visit, just swap the school name and date.

### Day 4 (Thursday) — Lesson plan: Visit 1 ("Meet Millie")

**Total run time: 45 minutes** (one class period). Designed for K-2.

| Time | Activity | What you say / do |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival & introduction | "I'm Stacie, this is my pony. Her name is _____. She's traveled all the way from Athol, Idaho to meet you." Indigenous land acknowledgment in one sentence. |
| 0:05–0:15 | Pony anatomy + 5 vocab words | Point to the pony. Teach: hoof, mane, withers, hay, gentle. Kids repeat each word and touch their own body where the pony's body part would be (mane = hair, withers = shoulders). |
| 0:15–0:30 | Sight word relay | 6 cards on the floor with sight words (the, and, was, you, said, are). Kids line up. One at a time, walk a few steps with the pony, stop at a card, read it out loud. Pony "delivers" the word. |
| 0:30–0:40 | Story time | You read aloud a short Native-authored children's book (recommendation: *Jingle Dancer* by Cynthia Leitich Smith, *We Are Water Protectors* by Carole Lindstrom, or *Fry Bread* by Kevin Noble Maillard). Class sits in a semicircle around the pony. |
| 0:40–0:45 | Photo time | Each kid takes a 10-second photo with the pony. You snap it on your phone with the school's name overlay already pre-loaded. |

**Materials checklist for visit:**
- [ ] Pony (well-groomed, has eaten, has had a bathroom break before going on campus)
- [ ] Halter + lead rope
- [ ] Hoof boots or shavings/mat for indoor surface (school floors are slippery)
- [ ] Cleanup kit: bag, gloves, disinfectant wipes, paper towels
- [ ] Sight word cards (6 cards, laminated, A4 size)
- [ ] One copy of the read-aloud book
- [ ] Phone with photo overlay app pre-set
- [ ] Insurance certificate + signed waivers binder
- [ ] Tribal Cowboy business cards / one-pagers for teachers and admin
- [ ] Carrot or apple treats (only if school approves)

### Day 5 (Friday) — Build the photo delivery flow

The photo delivery is the single most important marketing asset you produce.

**Same-day delivery flow:**
1. After the visit, batch all photos through your overlay tool (you can do this in the school parking lot)
2. Upload to a **single shared gallery** on your facial-recognition photo platform — title it: "Mrs. [Teacher Name]'s Class — [School] — [Date]"
3. Generate ONE QR code per class that links to that gallery
4. Email the teacher within 2 hours of the visit with:
   - Thank you note (3 sentences max)
   - The QR code as a PNG attachment
   - A short paragraph for the teacher to email home to parents:

```
Hi families — today our class had a wonderful visit from Tribal Cowboy
and their pony, [Pony Name], as part of a Saddle Up & Read program pilot.
Every student got their photo taken with the pony. To find your child's
photo, scan the QR code below or visit tribalcowboy.com/[school-slug].
There's no cost — these are a gift from Tribal Cowboy.
```

5. Parents scan, see the branded photo, can download it free, are now in your audience

## Week 2 — Target school selection + outreach

### The pilot target list (these are easiest to say yes)

**Tier A — Easiest yes (start here):**
These don't require district approval. A single principal or co-op leader can green-light a visit.

| Type | Specific targets in North Idaho | Why they're easy |
|---|---|---|
| **Homeschool co-ops** | Cabrini Homeschool Co-op (CDA), Arrows Homeschool Co-op (CDA), Inland Northwest Christian Homeschoolers, CHAIN Homeschoolers | Volunteer-led, decisions made by one or two parents, love enrichment |
| **Private schools** | Holy Family Catholic School (CDA), Classical Christian Academy (Post Falls), Lake City Junior Academy | Single decision-maker (principal), strong PTA, looser bureaucracy |
| **Charter schools** | North Idaho STEM Charter (Rathdrum), Coeur d'Alene Charter Academy | More flexible than traditional public, often hunting for unique programming |
| **Libraries** | Community Library Network (Hayden, Rathdrum, Post Falls), CDA Public Library | Story time slots are easy to book, librarians love this stuff |

**Tier B — Bigger yes, requires more steps (target after pilots succeed):**
- Public school districts: CDA #271, Lakeland #272, Post Falls #273, Kootenai #274
- These need approval from a curriculum coordinator or enrichment coordinator. You'll need your pilot case study to get in the door.

### Recommended pilot mix (Sprint + Heritage):
1. **One homeschool co-op for the Sprint** — easiest yes, parents will rave online, 4 weekly visits, real learning outcomes
2. **One private school OR library for the Heritage Visit** — single 60-min visit, tests the one-time format as a low-friction entry point that converts schools to next year's Sprint customers

### The exact outreach email

Send this to each target. Personalize the first paragraph only.

```
Subject: Free pilot visit — pony + literacy program for your students

Hi [Name],

My name is Stacie Hines. I run Tribal Cowboy, an Indigenous-owned equine
business in Athol — we work with North Idaho schools, libraries, and
families with our ponies and Clydesdales.

We're piloting a new free literacy program called Saddle Up & Read,
and I'm looking for two North Idaho classrooms (or your co-op) to test
it with this spring. The visit is 45 minutes, comes to your location,
costs nothing, and is built around K-2 sight words. We bring a gentle
miniature pony, read a Native-authored children's book together, and
every student leaves with a photo of themselves with the pony, delivered
to parents the same day at no cost.

This is modeled on programs running in Memphis, Dallas, and Massachusetts
that have shown real engagement gains for early readers. I'm Nisenan
Maidu, and part of the program includes a short cultural lesson on the
arrival of horses on the Plateau.

I have liability insurance, signed waivers, and references. If you're
open to hosting one of the pilot visits in the next 30 days, I'd love
a 15-minute call to walk you through it.

Stacie Hines
Tribal Cowboy
208-XXX-XXXX
stacie@tribalcowboy.com
```

### Follow-up cadence
- Day 0: Send email
- Day 3: If no reply, send a one-line follow-up: *"Bumping this up — happy to send a one-pager if it's easier than reading the email."*
- Day 7: Call the front office. *"Hi, I emailed about a free pony visit pilot — is the principal the right person, or should I send to someone else?"*
- Day 14: Move on. Don't chase.

### How to make it irresistible — sweeteners

Mention these in the call (not the email — keep the email short):
- **Aligned to Idaho ELA K-2 standards:** Reading Foundations (RF.K.3, RF.1.3), Speaking & Listening (SL.K.1)
- **Cultural component supports** Idaho social studies indigenous studies curriculum
- **You can also bring a teacher resource packet** (sight word cards they keep, a follow-up reading list)
- **You'll provide a class photo printed and framed** as a thank-you to the teacher

That last one — the framed class photo — is the kindest detail. Costs you $15. Teachers will remember you forever.

## Week 3 — Run the pilots

### 3 days before the visit
- Confirm time, room, and entry instructions with the teacher
- Email the photo release packet to the teacher with a request to send home
- Check the weather (if outdoors)
- Wash the pony

### Day of the visit
- Arrive 30 minutes early
- Park where directed; do NOT walk the pony through halls if you can avoid it (find a side door)
- Walk-around with the pony before kids arrive so the pony gets used to the room
- Set out the sight word cards
- Greet teacher by name, hand them your business card and a small thank-you note

### After the visit (within 2 hours)
- Process and overlay photos
- Upload to gallery
- Email teacher with QR code and parent-friendly blurb
- Post one photo to Instagram with the school's permission

### Within 24 hours
- Text the teacher a personal thank-you
- Save 3 best photos to your case-study folder
- Ask the teacher for a one-paragraph testimonial via email

### Within 7 days
- Track photo gallery views and downloads (parent engagement data)
- Note: how many parents downloaded? How many shared? Did anyone DM you afterwards?
- Save EVERYTHING in a folder called `pilot-1-[school-name]/`

## Mistakes to avoid (from the other operators' visible bruises)

1. **Don't try to charge schools.** Mobile Whinnies, Equest, Southern Reins, and Gentle Carousel all give it away free. Schools will say no instantly to a fee. Sponsors pay.

2. **Don't bring a 1,800-lb Clydesdale to a classroom for the first pilot.** Use a calm, well-trained pony or mini. Save Millie for the *field-trip-to-the-farm* visit (visit 4 in the residency model). Indoor visits = mini or small pony only.

3. **Don't skip the photo release.** Even one parent who didn't want their kid photographed can sink the program. The release form is non-negotiable.

4. **Don't pitch the residency model on the pilot.** Pitch a single free visit. Once the school loves it, *then* propose the 4-visit residency for the next year.

5. **Don't post pilot photos publicly without the photo release box checked.** This is the single fastest way to destroy your reputation with local schools.

6. **Don't forget the teacher gift.** Teachers are your repeat customers. The framed class photo is the cheapest goodwill purchase you'll ever make.

7. **Don't go alone.** Bring one helper — a calm adult who can manage the pony while you teach, or vice versa. A volunteer parent works. Solo with a pony + 25 kids is too many things at once.

## The one thing that makes this work

Treat the pilot like a **proof, not a launch.** You're not trying to scale yet. You're trying to generate ONE undeniable piece of evidence — a photo of a 6-year-old who learned to read the word "and" with a pony's nose six inches from their face — and TWO sentences from a teacher.

With those two assets, every door opens.

Without them, every door is half-closed.
