The Pony Post is an **Equine-Assisted Learning (EAL) program** — an educational literacy program built around a calm pony. It is **NOT** therapy, counseling, mental health treatment, or a substitute for any of those services. Activities support reading, vocabulary, phonological awareness, and classroom focus. They do not diagnose or treat any condition. Idaho regulates the practice of counseling, therapy, and psychology — The Pony Post stays firmly on the educational side of that line.

## The Research Behind This Program

The themed Pre-K/K Sprint is designed against three converging bodies of research. Each week and every activity below is mapped to a specific finding.

### 1. The National Reading Panel — Five Pillars of Reading (2000)

The National Reading Panel synthesized 100,000+ studies and identified five evidence-based pillars of reading instruction. The Pony Post hits all five across four weeks.

| Pillar | Where it lives in this Sprint |
|---|---|
| **Phonemic & phonological awareness** | Week 2 (syllable counting with the pony's hoof) and Week 4 (rhyming refrains) |
| **Phonics** | Week 1 (letters in their own name) and Week 3 (initial-sound matching) |
| **Fluency** | Repeated, predictable text in every custom book — read aloud at the visit, re-read at home with parents |
| **Vocabulary** | 5 anchor words per week (20 total) + 4 weeks of themed content vocabulary |
| **Comprehension** | Week 4 — kids retell the four-week story, identify characters, sequence events |

### 2. The National Early Literacy Panel (2008) — Predictors of Later Reading

NELP identified six skills measured in Pre-K/K that predict reading achievement through grade 3. **The Pony Post directly targets all six.**

| NELP Predictor | How the Sprint builds it |
|---|---|
| **Alphabet knowledge** | Week 1 — kids write their own name on an envelope; Week 4 — first-letter matching with the pony |
| **Phonological awareness** | Week 2 — syllable claps with the pony's hoof |
| **Rapid automatic naming of letters/digits** | Week 3 — counting silver pieces 1–7 with the pony |
| **RAN of objects/colors** | Week 2 — *Pony, Pony, What Do You See?* color and animal naming |
| **Writing one's own name** | Week 1 — write your name on the envelope to mail back to the pony |
| **Phonological memory** | Every week — repeating refrains in the read-alouds + the Saddle Up/Whoa Partners callbacks |

This matters: writing one's own name is one of the **strongest single predictors** of K reading success. We build it into Week 1 as the centerpiece activity.

### 3. The Science of Reading & Scarborough's Reading Rope (2001)

Scarborough's framework shows skilled reading as two woven strands:

- **Language Comprehension** — background knowledge, vocabulary, language structures, verbal reasoning, literacy knowledge
- **Word Recognition** — phonological awareness, decoding, sight word recognition

Pre-K/K instruction must build BOTH strands simultaneously. The Pony Post's themed weeks each lead with a different strand, and most weeks hit both.

| Week | Lead strand | Skills built |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 — Pony Express Post | Word Recognition | Print awareness, letter recognition, name writing |
| Week 2 — Pony, Pony, What Do You See? | Language Comprehension | Vocabulary, predictable text, color/animal naming |
| Week 3 — Pit Pony | Word Recognition + Background Knowledge | Counting, days-of-week, Idaho history |
| Week 4 — Detective Pony | Language Comprehension | Inference, sequencing, story retelling |

### 4. Supporting Research — Why Animals Work for Early Literacy

- **Dialogic reading** (Whitehurst et al., 1988): Interactive read-alouds with prompts and feedback can produce **6-month vocabulary gains** in Pre-K in as little as 4–6 weeks. Every Pony Post read-aloud uses dialogic technique.
- **Animal-assisted reading** (e.g., R.E.A.D. — Reading Education Assistance Dogs, founded 1999, peer-reviewed): Children who read aloud to animals show measurable gains in fluency, accuracy, and reading attitude. The non-judging audience lowers anxiety and increases time-on-task.
- **Repeated reading** (LaBerge & Samuels, 1974): Children re-reading the same text grow fluency faster than children reading new text each time. We send each book home so families can re-read all month.
- **Multisensory learning** (Orton-Gillingham foundation, validated repeatedly): Pairing sight + sound + touch + movement (the syllable stomp, the hoof paw, the costume reveal) strengthens retention vs. single-channel instruction.

### 5. The Mailbag Mechanic — Why Letters Build Engagement

Pen-pal correspondence between visits is the program's signature mechanic. The research backs it:

- **Authentic audience writing** (Routman, Atwell): Children write more, write longer, and write better when there's a real reader.
- **Between-session continuity** (spaced retrieval — Karpicke & Roediger, 2007): Material recalled at spaced intervals is retained ~2x longer than material reviewed all at once. Weekly letters trigger that retrieval naturally.
- **Affective engagement** (Pianta, classroom relationships research): Kids who feel personally known by a teacher (or, here, a pony) attend more, participate more, and retain more.

## Core Mechanics (Same Every Week)

### The Visual Schedule
A felt board with 6 laminated picture cards. Move a clip down the board as the visit progresses.

[👋 Hello] → [✋ 3 Rules] → [🐴 Pony Looks] → [📖 Story] → [✍️ Make Something] → [👋 Goodbye]

### The Two Callbacks

**🐴 Saddle Up / Pony Hooves** (Energy UP):
- Stacie: *"Saddle up!"*
- Kids: *"Giddy up!"* + stomp 3 times + Pony Hooves (fists, knuckles up, near tummy)

**🤫 Whoa Partners / Pony Ears** (Energy DOWN):
- Stacie: *"Whoa, partners!"*
- Kids: Freeze + cup hands behind ears

### The 3 Big Rules — Recited at every visit
1. **Inside voice.** Ponies are prey animals.
2. **Walk, don't run.** Quick movement scares horses.
3. **Tell the pony you're there.** Say *"Hi, pony"* — especially behind her.

### Pony Looks — 6 body language signals
Happy ears, grumpy ears, calm eyes, scared eyes, quiet tail, mad tail. (See `pony-looks-flip-cards-spec.md` — already produced.)

### Pony Pals Sticker Book (take-home, distributed Visit 1, completed across all 4 weeks)
4-page stapled mini-book. Each week: a sticker + the new anchor word + a drawing space.

# Week 1 — Pony Express Post 📬

**Theme:** The pony has a job. She delivers mail. We write our names on letters and mail them back.

**Pony Costume — Pony Express Rider:**
- Leather or canvas mailbag stamped "U.S. MAIL" or "PONY EXPRESS"
- Red bandana around the neck
- Small sheriff-style star badge clipped to the breast collar
- Optional: small cowboy hat clipped to the bridle

**Custom Book — *A Letter For You***

**Modeled on:** *The Jolly Postman* (Janet & Allan Ahlberg) — letters delivered to recipients on each spread, with the predictable repetition of *Click Clack Moo* (Doreen Cronin). This is an original work using a public-domain repeating-text pattern with Stacie's photos and her own ranch. Do not replicate either book's exact text or art.

**Full draft text — *A Letter For You*** (12 pages, ~60 words):

| Page | Photo | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Pony with mailbag, ranch gate behind her | *A LETTER FOR YOU — A Pony Post Story* |
| 1 | Pony at the barn, morning light | *The pony has a job.* |
| 2 | Pony walking, mailbag on her side | *She carries the mail.* |
| 3 | Close-up of envelopes in the bag | *The mail has letters.* |
| 4 | Pony walking toward camera | *Knock, knock!* |
| 5 | Pony at a fence, "looking" at the reader | *Who is there?* |
| 6 | Pony with one envelope showing | *It is the pony.* |
| 7 | Envelope close-up with "FOR YOU" stamped | *She has a letter for YOU.* |
| 8 | Wide shot of the open envelope | *The letter says:* |
| 9 | Letter text in handwritten font | *"Hi, friend. I am a pony. Love, the pony."* |
| 10 | Blank envelope insert (real one glued in) | *Now you write a letter back.* |
| 11 | Pony walking away, mailbag empty | *The pony will come back.* |
| 12 | Pony at the gate, looking back | *See you soon!* |

**Why it works for Pre-K/K:** Repetitive structure (*knock knock / who is there / it is the pony*) builds predictable text fluency. The letter inside the book is a real envelope kids can open — print concepts in the most concrete form possible. Final page reverses the lens onto the child as a writer.

**Run-of-Show — Pre-K (30 min) / K (45 min)**

| Time (PreK / K) | Activity |

| 0:08–0:14 / 0:09–0:18 | **Meet the Pony Express Rider.** Notice the mailbag. *"She has a job today."* Walk around the pony — demo Pony Vision blind spots (front of nose, behind tail). Touch from the SIDE. |
| 0:14–0:20 / 0:18–0:28 | **Read-aloud:** *A Letter For You.* Open the real envelope at page 10. Read the letter inside aloud. Dialogic prompts: *"Who is the letter from? What does it say? Who else gets a letter?"* |
| 0:20–0:27 / 0:28–0:40 | **Write Your Name on an Envelope.** Each kid gets a real envelope and a crayon. Pre-K: write the first letter of their name + draw themselves. K: write their full name + draw the pony. "Mail" the envelope by tucking it into the pony's mailbag. Pony "delivers" them home with the teacher at the end. |
| 0:27–0:30 / 0:40–0:45 | **Body language check** ("What do her ears say right now?") + Sticker for the Pony Pals book + Goodbye |

# Week 3 — Pip the Pit Pony of the Silver Valley ⛏️

**Theme:** A long time ago in our state, brave little ponies worked in the silver mines. We count their days and their silver. Small can mean strong.

**Why this week matters:** The Silver Valley around Kellogg, Idaho was one of the world's largest silver-producing regions. Real pit ponies hauled ore carts in the Bunker Hill and Sunshine mines. **This is local history.** Teachers and principals will love that the program teaches Idaho heritage alongside ELA — especially in North Idaho schools.

**Pony Costume — Pip the Miner:**
- Small **headlamp** (LED clip-on, kid-safe, off during read-aloud) attached to the bridle
- **Sooty bandana** — a gray bandana around the neck (don't actually dirty her — use a pre-washed gray cloth)
- A **"Silver Valley Mining Co." sash** across her chest (felt or canvas, hand-painted)
- Optional: a small toy mine cart on wheels, pulled behind on a soft rope, loaded with foil-wrapped "silver" rocks

**Custom Book — *Pip the Pit Pony***

**Modeled on:** *The Very Hungry Caterpillar* by Eric Carle (1969) — counting + days of the week + small-thing-grows-strong arc. Original text by Stacie; original photos of the actual ranch pony in costume. Setting based on the real Silver Valley.

| 3 | Close-up of the headlamp | *Pip wears a light. The mine is dark.* |

# Week 4 — Detective Pony 🔍

**Theme:** The pony is a detective. We are detectives too. We follow clues. We retell the whole story. We say goodbye.

**Pony Costume — Sherlock Pony:**
- **Deerstalker hat** (Sherlock Holmes style) strapped to the bridle
- **Bow tie** at the breast collar (formal!)
- **Magnifying glass** prop hanging from the saddle
- **Cardboard clue tags** dangling from the bridle, each with one mystery word
- A "DETECTIVE PONY" sash (or badge across the saddle pad)

**Custom Book — *Detective Pony and the Missing Apple***

**Modeled on:** *Pete the Cat: I Love My White Shoes* by Eric Litwin (HarperCollins, 2010) for the repetitive call-and-response refrain, with a touch of *Where's Spot?* (Eric Hill, 1980) for the "look and find" mechanic. Original text and photos by Stacie.

**Full draft text — *Detective Pony and the Missing Apple*** (14 pages, ~90 words):

| Page | Photo | Text |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | Pony in deerstalker hat with magnifying glass | *DETECTIVE PONY AND THE MISSING APPLE* |
| 1 | Pony in the barn, apple on a hay bale | *There was an apple in the barn.* |
| 2 | Same scene, apple GONE | *The apple is GONE!* |
| 3 | Pony with magnifying glass | *Detective Pony will find it.* |
| 4 | Pony looking UP at a beam | *Detective Pony looked HIGH.* |
| 5 | Pony looking DOWN at the floor | *Detective Pony looked LOW.* |
| 6 | Kitten on a hay bale | *Did the kitten take it?* |
| 7 | Kitten close-up | *"Not me!" said the kitten.* |
| 8 | Puppy in the yard | *Did the puppy take it?* |
| 9 | Puppy close-up | *"Not me!" said the puppy.* |
| 10 | Chicken in the coop | *Did the chicken take it?* |
| 11 | Chicken close-up | *"Not me!" said the chicken.* |
| 12 | Pony with apple core in mouth or beside her | *...The pony ate the apple!* |
| 13 | Pony with big happy eye | *Case closed!* |
| 14 | Pony at the pasture gate, looking back | *Goodbye, friends.* |

**Why it works for Pre-K/K:** The repeating *"Did the X take it? 'Not me!' said the X"* refrain is exactly the predictable text pattern that builds fluency through repetition. The surprise reveal makes kids laugh — laughter is memory glue. The closing *"Goodbye, friends"* gives emotional closure to the whole 4-week relationship.

**Run-of-Show — Pre-K (30 min) / K (45 min)**

| Time (PreK / K) | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 / 0:00–0:05 | Hello + Saddle Up. Notice the deerstalker hat. *"What is the pony today?"* |
| 0:03–0:06 / 0:05–0:08 | 3 Rules + body language check (last time!) |
| 0:06–0:14 / 0:08–0:20 | **Read-aloud:** *Detective Pony and the Missing Apple.* Every kid has their own copy. Class chants the *"Not me!"* refrain together. Dialogic prompts: *"Where did the pony look? Who did she ask? Who took it?!"* |
| 0:14–0:20 / 0:20–0:30 | **🔍 Clue Hunt + Detective Badge.** Each kid gets a Detective Badge (cardstock badge with a magnifying-glass shape). They write their name on it. Then a worksheet: 5 scenes, each with a small apple hidden. Kids use a real magnifying glass to find each apple. K kids can label each scene with a first letter (B for barn, T for tree). |
| 0:20–0:26 / 0:30–0:40 | **🐴 Retell the Sprint.** Stacie holds up all 4 books in order. *"Who can tell me what happened in book one? Book two? Book three? Book four?"* Kids take turns retelling. (K.RL.2) Then a rhyme game: *"What rhymes with PONY? With MAIL? With PIP? With BARN?"* (K.RF.2.a) |
| 0:26–0:30 / 0:40–0:45 | **Final Goodbye.** The pony bows (if trained). Final sticker + Reader's Pin attached to the Pony Pals Sticker Book. Photo with the pony for each kid (school-approved photo release). Wave goodbye. |

# Why This Sprint Works

1. **Theme variety keeps Pre-K/K attention** — every week is a new costume, a new book, a new world
2. **Predictable structure under the theme variety** — same visual schedule, same callbacks, same safety rules at every visit
3. **Letters between visits** — kids retrieve and rehearse the previous week's content every Monday morning, free
4. **Standards alignment is real, not decorative** — every activity earns a standard
5. **Local heritage** — Pit Pony week ties the program to Idaho's silver mining story
6. **Books go home** — four take-home books per kid, sponsor logo on every back cover
7. **The pony is the lesson** — calmness, predictability, sensory grounding — research-backed reading conditions, built around an animal

The signature moves:

- **Week 1's envelope** — the first time many Pre-K/K kids write their own name for an authentic audience (a real pony will "read" their letter)
- **Week 2's syllable hoof paws** — phonological awareness through movement, anchored to an animal's body
- **Week 3's local Idaho history** — the only mining-history Pre-K reading program in the state, and it ends in a hug
- **Week 4's mystery** — laughter is memory glue, and the pony eating the apple is the moment kids tell their grandparents about

Build those four moves well and the program teaches itself.
