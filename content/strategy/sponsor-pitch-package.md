- **Modeled on proven national programs** — Mobile Whinnies (MA), Equest (TX), Southern Reins (TN), Gentle Carousel (FL)

## 3. The 15-Minute In-Person Pitch Flow

If they say yes to a meeting. Bring: tier sheet (printed), a pilot case study one-pager (after pilots are done), one printed Hay Budget Booklet and Pony Passport sample, business cards, and a small jar of horse treats (always memorable). 15 minutes only — respect their time.

### Minute 0–2: Open with the why
- Thank them for the time
- One-sentence about Tribal Cowboy: *"I'm an Indigenous-owned equine business in Athol. I bring ponies and Clydesdales to schools and families across North Idaho."*
- One-sentence about the problem: *"There's a real literacy gap in early grades up here, and most families don't have the budget for the kind of one-on-one enrichment that closes it."*

### Minute 2–6: Show the program
- Walk them through one Sprint (Reading with Millie OR Penny's Hay Budget — pick the one that fits the sponsor)
- Show the materials: the booklet, the take-home one-pager (point out where THEIR logo would go), a pen-pal letter from the pony
- Mention the pilot results (after you have them): *"We piloted this at [School] in May and the teacher said [quote]. Twenty kids went from recognizing 5 sight words to recognizing 18 in four weeks."*
- Mention the bigger picture: *"This model works — Mobile Whinnies in Massachusetts and Equest in Dallas have been running it for years."*

### Minute 6–10: Show what they get
- Hand them the tier sheet
- Walk them through the four tiers
- Identify which tier fits their giving budget: *"For [Company], the Sprint Sponsor at $2,000 is the natural fit — it gets your logo on the take-home one-pager that goes to about 60 family members per Sprint."*
- Mention the photo deliverable: *"You'll get a small library of program photos you can use in your own community-impact reports and social media."*

### Minute 10–13: Make the ask
- Direct, clear, no hedging: *"Would [Company] be open to sponsoring one Sprint this fall? It's $2,000. It serves one school. I'd love to put you with [target school name] in [target neighborhood]."*
- Then SHUT UP. Let them respond. Most people fill silence with yes.

### Minute 13–15: Close
- If yes: *"Wonderful. I'll send a one-page agreement this afternoon. Can we plan to launch in [month]? What's the best way to coordinate logo files and your team's input?"*
- If maybe: *"Totally understand. What information would help you make the decision? I'll send anything you need by tomorrow."*
- If no: *"Thanks for being honest about that. Would [Company] be a fit at the $500 Friend tier or for a one-time Heritage Visit? If not now, would it make sense to revisit in [Q3 / next budget cycle]?"*
- Always: *"Thank you so much for your time. I'll follow up by email tomorrow."*

## 7. The Sponsorship Math (How to Pitch the Numbers)

When sponsors push back on $2,000, here's the value math to walk them through:

**For $2,000, you serve:**
- ~20 students directly
- ~60 family members reached via take-home one-pager
- 4 weeks × 1 visit = 4 brand impressions per kid in the classroom
- 1 take-home one-pager × 60 family members = 60 home-level brand impressions
- 1 sponsor-branded pen-pal letter per kid × 3 letters = 60 between-visit impressions
- Total: ~140 brand impressions to families and ~80 in the classroom for $2,000
- Plus: a library of 30+ usable program photos for sponsor's own marketing
- Plus: social media features reaching Tribal Cowboy's audience (~1,000+ NI families)
- Plus: tax-deductible donation through 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsor

**Per-impression cost:** roughly $2,000 / 220 impressions = **about $9 per family-level impression**, which is competitive with even targeted Facebook advertising — but with the trust value of "sponsor of a school program" instead of "company that bought an ad."

This is the number that closes hesitating sponsors. Use it.

## 9. What to Do When a Sponsor Says...

**"We don't have a budget for this right now."**
> *Totally understand. May I check back during your next budget cycle? In the meantime, would your team be open to a free farm visit with your families — no obligation? It's a way to introduce ourselves and stay connected.*

**"$2,000 is more than we usually give."**
> *I get it. We have a $500 Friend tier that funds a single Heritage Visit — that gets your logo in front of about 30 families and is a great way to test the partnership before committing to a full Sprint. Would that be a better fit for this year?*

**"We'd want exclusivity in our category."**
> *Happy to work that out. The Title Sponsor tier at $5,000 includes category exclusivity for the Sprint we run together — so you'd be the only [bank / hospital / etc.] on those materials.*

**"We need to think about it."**
> *Of course. What information would help you decide? I'll send anything you need this week. Can I check back in 10 days?*

**"Can you make this match our brand colors?"**
> *Absolutely. Send me your brand guidelines and I'll mock up the take-home materials with your colors and logo before we finalize anything. You'll get full approval on every piece your name appears on.*

**"What about ROI?"**
> *Real numbers: $2,000 reaches about 220 family-level brand impressions plus 30+ usable photos for your marketing plus social media plus tax deduction. That's about $9 per family impression, which is competitive with paid social media advertising — but with the trust value of being a school program sponsor instead of an ad.*

**"Have you done this before?"**
> *We're piloting in 2026. The model is proven nationally — Mobile Whinnies in Massachusetts, Equest in Dallas, Southern Reins in Memphis — and our pilots so far show [X% sight word improvement / strong teacher feedback / specific quote]. We're being very intentional about Year 1 sponsors because you become the foundation of the program.*

## 10. The Most Important Mindset

You are not asking for charity. You are offering a service that solves a specific problem the sponsor already has on their list — community visibility, financial literacy education, brand goodwill, content for their own marketing. Banks have community-giving budgets they MUST spend every year. Hospitals have early childhood priorities. Casinos have mandated community-giving requirements.

You're not begging. You're offering them the best community-impact deal in the region for the money. Walk into every meeting believing that, and the meetings change.

The first three sponsorships are the hardest. After that, sponsors talk to each other and they start to come to you.
