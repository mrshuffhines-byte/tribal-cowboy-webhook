# Pony Looks Flip Cards — Print-Ready Spec
## 6 Body Language Cards for The Pony Post Visit 1
### Tribal Cowboy LLC — Athol, Idaho

**Purpose:** Six oversized two-sided flip cards Stacie holds up during the **Pony Looks + Synonyms** block of Visit 1 (Pre-K and K-2). The front shows a real photo of the pony making the look + the kid-facing word. The back shows Stacie's teaching script and notes.

**Used at:** Every Pony Post visit (primary at Visit 1, reinforcement at Visits 2–4).

**Reusable:** One deck lasts for years. Stacie ships them home in a small case in the truck.

---

| Spec | Recommendation |

## The 6 Cards

Each card is two-sided. **Front = what the kids see.** **Back = what Stacie reads/glances at.**

---

### Card 1 — HAPPY EARS / Interested

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
A clean shot of the pony's head and shoulders, ears pointed clearly **forward** toward the camera or toward something out of frame. Ears should be the most visible feature — sharp focus, clear background. Take this shot when the pony is greeted, fed, or sees something she likes.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (full-bleed)
- Bottom half (white space): big bold text
  - **HAPPY EARS** (large, bold sans-serif, ~120pt)
  - *Another word: INTERESTED* (medium italic, ~50pt)
- Small Tribal Cowboy logo in the bottom-right corner

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** HAPPY EARS / INTERESTED

**What it looks like:** Ears pointing forward, toward whatever the pony is paying attention to.

**What it means:** Curious. Friendly. Paying attention. The pony is comfortable.

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Look at her ears. Pointing FORWARD. She's curious. She's interested in something. That's HAPPY ears. Another word for HAPPY is INTERESTED. Say it with me — HAPPY EARS, INTERESTED."*

**Optional body action for kids:** Hands above their head with fingers pointed forward like ears.

---

### Card 2 — GRUMPY EARS / Mad ⚠️ Warning Sign

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
The pony with ears **pinned flat back** against her head/neck. This is harder to photograph because a school-trained pony rarely does this — but you can catch it briefly when she's annoyed at a fly, when another horse approaches her hay, or when she's just been moved off her favorite spot. **Alternative:** photograph the pony with ears halfway back (mildly annoyed) and label it. Don't try to provoke her for the photo.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (full-bleed)
- Bottom half: big bold text
  - **GRUMPY EARS** (large, bold)
  - *Another word: MAD* (italic)
  - **⚠️ WARNING SIGN** (small red badge in the corner)
- Small Tribal Cowboy logo bottom-right

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** GRUMPY EARS / MAD ⚠️ Warning Sign #1

**What it looks like:** Ears flat back, pressed against her head/neck — like she folded them shut.

**What it means:** She's saying *"leave me alone."* Give her space. **This is one of the 3 Warning Signs that says STOP, step back, tell a grown-up.**

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Ears FLAT against her head — GRUMPY ears. She's saying 'leave me alone.' Another word for GRUMPY is MAD."*

> *"This is a WARNING sign. If you see flat ears, STOP. Don't get closer. Tell a grown-up."*

**Optional body action:** Hands flat against the sides of their head, palms covering ears.

---

### Card 3 — CALM EYES / Relaxed

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
Close-up of the pony's eye — soft, half-closed or blinking, gentle. Best shot when she's chewing hay, dozing in the sun, or being brushed. Eye should look soft and round, not wide.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (full-bleed, close-up of eye)
- Bottom half: big bold text
  - **CALM EYES** (large, bold)
  - *Another word: RELAXED* (italic)
- Small logo bottom-right

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** CALM EYES / RELAXED

**What it looks like:** Eye is soft, half-closed, gentle. Long blinks. No tension around the eye.

**What it means:** The pony is at peace. Comfortable. Happy where she is.

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Soft eyes. Gentle blinking. That's CALM eyes. Another word for CALM is RELAXED. Say it — CALM EYES, RELAXED."*

**Optional body action:** Kids blink slowly three times.

---

### Card 4 — SCARED EYES / Afraid

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
The pony with **wide eyes** — you can see the **white** part around the iris (called "whites of her eyes"). This shot is hardest to get with a calm school pony — you might catch it briefly when something startles her (a sudden noise, an unfamiliar dog, a flapping tarp). **If you can't get this shot of your own pony,** you can use a different horse photo for this one card. Note in the back that this is a reference image.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (full-bleed)
- Bottom half: big bold text
  - **SCARED EYES** (large, bold)
  - *Another word: AFRAID* (italic)
- Small logo bottom-right

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** SCARED EYES / AFRAID

**What it looks like:** Wide-open eyes with the white part showing around the iris. Sometimes called "whites of her eyes."

**What it means:** Something has scared her. She's looking for a way to escape. **Back off. Give her space and quiet.**

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Wide eyes — you can see the WHITE part — SCARED eyes. Another word for SCARED is AFRAID."*

> *"If you see scared eyes, walk away slowly. Don't run. Give her quiet."*

**Optional body action:** Kids open their eyes very wide.

---

### Card 5 — QUIET TAIL / Calm

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
A clear shot of the pony's tail hanging naturally, still or gently swaying. Side-angle of the pony so her whole body is visible. Best shot on a calm day in the pasture.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (side-angle, tail visible)
- Bottom half: big bold text
  - **QUIET TAIL** (large, bold)
  - *Another word: CALM* (italic)
- Small logo bottom-right

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** QUIET TAIL / CALM

**What it looks like:** Tail hangs naturally, still or with slow gentle sway. No fast movement.

**What it means:** Everything's fine. She's comfortable. No bugs, no irritation, no worry.

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Tail not moving — that's QUIET tail. Everything's good. Another word for QUIET is CALM."*

**Optional body action:** Kids put one hand behind them (like a tail) and hold it still.

---

### Card 6 — MAD TAIL / Annoyed ⚠️ Warning Sign

#### FRONT (kid-facing)

**Photo direction:**
The pony's tail captured mid-swish — fast, hard. Sharp photo at the moment her tail is fully extended sideways or behind. This is the easiest "warning sign" photo to catch — ponies swish at flies all the time. Take 20 photos of her on a buggy day and pick the most dramatic one.

**Layout:**
- Top half: photo (showing the swishing motion clearly)
- Bottom half: big bold text
  - **MAD TAIL** (large, bold)
  - *Another word: ANNOYED* (italic)
  - **⚠️ WARNING SIGN** (small red badge in the corner)
- Small logo bottom-right

#### BACK (Stacie-facing)

**Title:** MAD TAIL / ANNOYED ⚠️ Warning Sign #2

**What it looks like:** Tail swishing fast and hard, like swatting a fly. Multiple swishes in a row.

**What it means:** Something is bothering her. Could be a fly. Could be a human getting too close. Could be a sound. **This is one of the 3 Warning Signs that says STOP, step back, tell a grown-up.**

**What to say to the kids:**
> *"Tail going FAST and HARD — like swatting a fly — that's MAD tail. She's annoyed. Another word for MAD is ANNOYED."*

> *"This is a WARNING sign. If you see a fast tail, STOP. Step back. Tell a grown-up."*

**Optional body action:** Kids wiggle one hand behind them like a fast-swishing tail.

---

## The 3rd Warning Sign — Lifted Foot

The third warning sign — **lifting a foot off the ground** — is **taught verbally, not with a card.** A school-trained pony almost never lifts a back leg in warning at a visit, so the card would rarely match a live moment. Instead, Stacie demonstrates with her own foot:

> *"The third warning sign — when a pony lifts ONE of her back feet off the ground, like this (lift your foot). That means she's getting ready to kick. If you ever see this, STOP. Walk away slowly. Tell a grown-up."*

You can add a Card 7 later if you want a visual reference (a photo of the pony with one back leg cocked). For the launch deck, 6 cards is enough.

## Production — How to Make Them

### Option A — DIY (cheapest, fastest)

1. **Take the photos** (one weekend at the ranch — see photo direction notes above). Take 5–10 photos per card so you have options.
2. **Lay out in Canva** — 8.5×11 portrait template. Front and back as separate pages. ~1 hour per card.
3. **Print at home or Staples** on heavy cardstock. ~$0.50 per page x 12 pages (6 cards × 2 sides) = $6.
4. **Laminate** with self-adhesive pouches (5 mil thickness or thicker, Amazon ~$15 for a 25-pack). Or use a thermal laminator if you have one.
5. **Hole-punch** the top-left corner. Add a 2-inch binder ring (~$5 for a pack of 10 on Amazon).

**Total cost: ~$30 for the first deck. ~$15 for each backup deck.**
**Time: One weekend to photograph + one weekend to lay out + one evening to print + laminate.**

### Option B — Office Depot or FedEx Office

Same Canva files. Upload as a print job. They'll print double-sided on cardstock + laminate.

- **Cost:** ~$5–$8 per card laminated = ~$30–$50 per deck
- **Time:** Drop off, pick up in 24–48 hours
- **Pro:** Cleaner finish, professional appearance
- **Con:** Slightly more expensive

### Option C — Custom flashcards from Vistaprint or Zazzle

Upload your Canva designs as a custom print product (no standard "flashcard" template, but you can use the "8.5×11 flyer" or "poster" product and select cardstock + lamination as add-ons).

- **Cost:** $40–$60 per deck
- **Time:** 7–10 days shipping
- **Pro:** Highest quality, ready to use out of the box
- **Con:** Slowest, most expensive

**My recommendation:** Start with Option A (DIY). Once you've used the cards through 2–3 Sprints and know which photos work, upgrade to Option B or C for a polished version.

## Canva Layout Spec (per card, for the DIY route)

### Front side (kid-facing)

- **Canvas:** 8.5 × 11 inches, portrait, white background
- **Top 60%:** Photo, full-bleed (no border). Drag in your photo, crop tight on the pony's relevant body part.
- **Middle 30%:** Big bold word
  - Font: **Fredoka One** or **Quicksand Bold** (both free in Canva)
  - Size: 100–120pt
  - Color: Dark brown (#3D2817) or black
- **Bottom 10%:** Synonym + logo
  - Synonym: italic 40pt
  - Tribal Cowboy logo: small, bottom-right corner
- **For Warning Sign cards (2 + 6):** Red ⚠️ badge in the top-right corner, "WARNING SIGN" text below

### Back side (Stacie-facing)

- **Canvas:** 8.5 × 11 inches, portrait, white background
- **Top:** Bold title (HAPPY EARS / INTERESTED) at 60pt
- **Body (single column):**
  - **What it looks like:** [text]
  - **What it means:** [text]
  - **What to say to the kids:** [italic quoted script]
  - **Optional body action:** [text]
- **Bottom:** Card number + Tribal Cowboy logo

## Storage & Transport

### In the truck

A small fabric carrying case OR a 9×12 padded envelope with a cardboard insert. Keep all 6 cards on the binder ring, stacked face-down so the deck top is Card 1 (HAPPY EARS).

### During the visit

- Hang the binder ring on a hook on Stacie's belt, or set the deck on a small easel where it's visible to her but not the kids.
- Flip in order: 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 (Happy Ears → Grumpy Ears → Calm Eyes → Scared Eyes → Quiet Tail → Mad Tail).
- After each card, lay it on a separate stack face-down so kids see only one at a time.

### Cleaning

Laminated cards wipe down with a damp cloth + mild soap. Don't soak. Don't fold.

## Photo Session Checklist

Schedule one weekend at the ranch to capture all the photos. Bring:

- [ ] Camera or phone with the best lens you have (iPhone Pro / Android equivalent is fine)
- [ ] A helper to hold treats and get the pony's attention from different angles
- [ ] Patience — you may need 30–50 photos to get 6 great ones
- [ ] Good lighting — morning or late afternoon, not midday glare
- [ ] Brushed, clean pony
- [ ] A simple background — fence, sky, barn wall — nothing busy

**Order of operations:**

1. **HAPPY EARS** — easy. Show her something interesting (treat, halter), shoot when ears point forward
2. **CALM EYES** — easy. Wait until she's relaxed (chewing hay), close-up on her eye
3. **QUIET TAIL** — easy. Side-angle shot when she's standing still
4. **MAD TAIL** — buggy day. Lots of swishing. Catch the motion.
5. **GRUMPY EARS** — hardest. Don't provoke her. Wait for a natural moment (another horse, a fly bothering her).
6. **SCARED EYES** — hardest. If you can't get a clean shot of your own pony, use a borrowed photo or a stock image and note it's a reference.

## Final Note

The flip cards are a small investment — about $30 and one weekend of work — that elevate every single Pony Post visit. Kids will remember the cards for the rest of their lives. Parents who see the cards in their kid's drawing or hear them recited at dinner will understand exactly what the program is teaching: **real horse-safety knowledge, dressed as a fun lesson.**

If you ever shoot a second photo session and want to expand to Cards 7–12, the layout template is reusable. The 6 core cards stay. The deck only grows.
