## Critical Setup — Animals Stay, Classes Rotate

You CANNOT walk three animals from classroom to classroom. They stay in ONE location all day. Classes come to them.

**Best location:** Gym OR outdoor covered area. Avoid carpet. Lay stall mats or tarps on hard floors.

**The setup:**
- Pony, horse, and Clydesdale in their own marked-off areas (15+ feet apart from each other so kids can see scale)
- Hay nets out of kid sight (let animals snack between sessions)
- Water buckets behind each animal
- Quiet zone rope or chair line 6+ feet from each animal
- Materials table at the entrance: take-home one-pagers, business cards, Sprint flyer

**The HELPER:** You need ONE adult helper for the day. Friend, family, or paid ($100–$150). Their job:
- Watch each animal's body language for stress
- Manage take-home materials at the door
- Hand out water/hay during breaks
- Take photos (no kid faces, wide shots only unless releases signed)
- Note-taker — write down kid quotes, teacher quotes, observations

## Animal Welfare Protocol (the non-negotiable)

Watch for stress signals on EACH animal:
- Tail swishing more than usual
- Ears pinned back
- Lifting feet anxiously
- Refusing treats or water
- Heavy sweating in cool air
- Sudden head-tossing or backing up

**If any animal shows signs:**
- Cut their current "feature time" — let them stand quietly in the back
- Other animals carry the session
- 30 min in a quiet space (trailer if possible)
- Water + hay

**Required breaks:**
- 20 min after 2nd Grade (10:55–11:30)
- 30 min for lunch (12:00–12:30)
- 5 min between every session

**Pick the RIGHT animals for this day:**
- Pony: the calmest, most kid-loving one
- Horse: same — chill, food-motivated, used to crowds
- Clydesdale: Abby (or whichever Clyde is most patient) — must be okay with kids touching her hooves

## Universal Opening — Use at the START of every session

This script works at every grade level. Adjust energy and vocabulary to the age but keep the structure.

**(0:00–0:03 — 3 minutes)**

> *"Hi everybody. I'm Stacie. I'm a horse person from a place called Tribal Cowboy in Athol. Today I brought THREE animals to meet you. This is [pony name] the pony. This is [horse name] the horse. And this — over here — is Abby, my Clydesdale. She's the biggest horse you'll ever see.*
>
> *Before anybody does anything, we have THREE rules. Just three. Hold up three fingers like me.*
>
> ***RULE ONE: Inside voice. No screaming. Horses are prey animals — a scream sounds like a wolf coming to hunt them.***
>
> ***RULE TWO: Walk, don't run. Quick moving things scare horses. Walk slow.***
>
> ***RULE THREE: If you walk near a horse, say HI first — especially behind her. Horses have blind spots and might get scared if they don't see you coming.***
>
> *Three rules. Inside voice. Walk. Say hi. Got it?"*

Kids: *"Yes!"*

> *"Good. Let me also teach you ONE quick signal. When I say 'WHOA, PARTNERS!' — you FREEZE and put your hands behind your ears like this (demo Pony Ears). That tells me you're ready to listen. Let's try it once. WHOA, PARTNERS!"*

Kids freeze + Pony Ears.

> *"Perfect. We'll use that any time it gets loud."*

That's 3 minutes. Same opening every session. Saves you from re-inventing each round.

# 3rd & 4th Grade Programs (history + math + body language)

## 1:30–1:55 — 3rd Grade (41 kids, 25 min)

**Theme:** Where horses came from + body language deeper.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Universal opening** |
| 0:03–0:10 | **How horses got to Idaho.** *"Horses are NOT from here. They came from a place called Spain hundreds of years ago, on big wooden ships across the ocean. When they got off the boats, they spread north all the way to Idaho. The Nez Perce tribe became famous for breeding spotted horses called Appaloosas. Riding a horse in Idaho is riding an animal whose great-great-great-grandparents came across an ocean."* |
| 0:10–0:15 | **Pony Vision lesson.** *"Watch this. The pony's eyes are on the SIDES of her head — not the front like ours. That means she can see ALMOST all the way around. But she has two blind spots: in front of her nose, and behind her tail. That's why we always say HI before we walk past her."* Walk slowly around the pony, narrating: *"Can she see me? Can she see me? Now I'm BEHIND her — she can't see me!"* |
| 0:15–0:20 | **Pony Looks — 6 signals.** Live demo with the real animals. Walk past each animal. *"What are her ears doing? Happy or grumpy?"* Same for eyes (calm or scared) and tail (quiet or mad). |
| 0:20–0:23 | **Q&A — call on 3 hands.** Whatever they ask, answer honestly. |
| 0:23–0:25 | **Goodbye + sticker.** Tease the 4-week Sprint. |

## 12:30–12:55 — 4th Grade (40 kids, 25 min)

**Theme:** Working horses + Hay Math.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Universal opening** |
| 0:03–0:08 | **Working horses of the American West.** Quick lesson: *"Horses have JOBS. Cowboys used them to round up cattle. The Pony Express used them to deliver mail across the country — teenage riders galloping for thousands of miles, switching horses every 10 miles. Today police still use horses on patrol."* Show the Clydesdale: *"These are draft horses. They pull WAGONS. They could pull a Civil War cannon. They could plow a whole field of wheat."* |
| 0:08–0:13 | **Pony Vision quick demo.** Same lesson as 3rd grade — eyes on the side, two blind spots. Show by walking around the pony. |
| 0:13–0:19 | **HAY BUDGET MATH on the board** (bring a small dry-erase board or whiteboard). *"The pony eats 1 flake of hay every day. The horse eats 3 flakes. The Clydesdale eats 5 flakes. How many flakes per day for all three together? (Answer: 9.) How many flakes in a week? (63.) How many in a year? (3,285.) That's a LOT of hay. Now — hay costs about $15 a bale. A bale has 12 flakes. How many bales do my horses eat in a year? Almost 275 bales. At $15 each, that's over $4,100 a year just on hay."* Kids' faces will light up. They love numbers about horse food. |
| 0:19–0:23 | **Q&A — what's the weirdest thing about working with horses?** Honest answers. |
| 0:23–0:25 | **Goodbye + sticker.** |
