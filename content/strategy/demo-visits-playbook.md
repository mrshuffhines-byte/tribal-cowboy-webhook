## The strategy in one paragraph

These are not "just" demo visits. They are two things at once. First, they are **live R&D** — you'll learn what works with 5-year-olds vs. 8-year-olds, what timing actually feels like in a real classroom, what activities flop and what activities hit, and what materials you actually need. Second, they are **proof-of-concept events** — every photo, every teacher quote, every parent quote becomes the evidence you'll use to sell the 4-week Sprint to the next ten schools. Treat every minute of these visits as if a sponsor were watching.

## Hayden Canyon Charter — Thursday Visit Plan

Three flexible options depending on the answers above. Pick the one that matches.

### Option A — Single class, K–2 (most likely)

**Format:** 45-minute classroom visit, similar to Visit 1 of the Reading Sprint
**Bring:** Daisy (your calm classroom pony)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival, brief land acknowledgment, introduce yourself + Daisy. *"I'm Stacie Hines, I'm Nisenan Maidu, and this is Daisy. She's a pony from a ranch in Athol. The land we're on today is the traditional home of the Schitsu'umsh people."* |
| 0:05–0:15 | Pony anatomy + 4 vocab words. Hoof, mane, withers, tail. Kids touch their own body where Daisy's body part would be. |
| 0:15–0:25 | Sight word relay (smaller version since it's a one-off). 5 cards: I, am, a, the, you. Each kid walks a few steps with Daisy (you hold the lead), stops at a card, reads it out loud. |

| 0:35–0:42 | Q&A — kids ask Daisy anything. You answer "as" Daisy in pony voice. |
| 0:42–0:45 | Goodbye + take-home one-pager for each kid to give parents. |

### Option B — Single class, 3rd–5th grade

**Format:** 45-minute classroom visit, more conversational, less sight-word-heavy
**Bring:** Daisy

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival + introduction with land acknowledgment |
| 0:05–0:15 | Pony care and history. The story of horses on the Plateau — Spanish ships, "elk dogs," the Nez Perce and the Appaloosa. Hands-on: how to brush a pony's mane, what hooves look like up close. |
| 0:15–0:25 | Hay budget math (preview of the Math Sprint). *"Daisy eats 1 flake of hay a day. A Clydesdale named Abby eats FIVE. How much do they eat together in a week? In a month? In a year?"* Group does the math on a whiteboard. |

| 0:35–0:42 | Q&A |
| 0:42–0:45 | Goodbye + take-home one-pager |

### Option C — School-wide assembly (multiple grades together)

**Format:** 30-minute assembly in the gym or outdoor courtyard
**Bring:** Daisy (small pony) — possibly a Clydesdale ONLY if outdoors

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival + land acknowledgment + Daisy introduction |
| 0:05–0:10 | Pony anatomy + history of horses on the Plateau (age-flexible) |
| 0:10–0:20 | Story circle. Read-aloud OR storytelling. Kids gather close. |
| 0:20–0:25 | Group Q&A — kids ask Daisy anything |
| 0:25–0:30 | Goodbye, hand each homeroom teacher a stack of take-home one-pagers to distribute |

**If outdoors AND Hayden Canyon approves:** Bring Abby the Clydesdale for the last 10 minutes as a special reveal. Kids will lose their minds. Worth it.

## Smart Start Learning Academy — June Visit Plan

Smart Start is a curriculum-based preschool, ages 2 through pre-K, multiple classrooms grouped by age. **This is a totally different audience than Hayden Canyon** — younger kids, shorter attention spans, more sensory exploration, NO sight words (developmentally inappropriate).

### The smart play

Pitch them on a **multi-classroom rotation visit**: you stay 2–3 hours, you visit each age group's classroom for 20–25 minutes. Each visit is tailored to that age group. You serve their entire program in one morning. They'll love this because it's like a special event day for the whole school.

### Lesson plan by age group

### For 2-year-olds (Guppies, Goldfish)
**20 minutes max. Pure sensory.**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival. Sit in a circle. Daisy stands quietly. Kids look from a safe distance. |
| 0:05–0:12 | Sensory exploration. With teacher supervision, each kid gets a 20-second turn to touch Daisy's mane, then her shoulder. Vocabulary: *soft, warm, gentle, pony*. Repeat each word together. |
| 0:12–0:18 | Read-aloud — a short, repetitive board book about horses or animals. Something like *Brown Bear, Brown Bear* (with a pony substituted into the rhyme by you). |
| 0:18–0:20 | Wave goodbye to Daisy. Teacher hands out a sticker of Daisy. |

### For 3-year-olds (Swordfish, Pufferfish, Starfish)
**25 minutes. Adds counting and naming.**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival. Land acknowledgment in one sentence. Daisy introduction. |
| 0:05–0:12 | Pony parts game. Point and name: hoof, mane, tail, eye, ear, nose. Kids repeat. Then: *"How many hooves does Daisy have?"* Count together to 4. *"How many eyes?"* Count to 2. |
| 0:12–0:20 | Read-aloud — short picture book with rhythm and repetition. Suggestions: *We Are Water Protectors* (simpler portions), *Bowwow Powwow* by Brenda J. Child, or any short horse-themed picture book. |
| 0:20–0:25 | Each kid gets a 15-second pony touch (under teacher supervision). Goodbye. Sticker. |

### For Pre-K (Dolphins, Sharks, Stingrays)
**30 minutes. Add early vocabulary and one early literacy connection.**

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Arrival + land acknowledgment + Daisy introduction |
| 0:05–0:15 | Pony anatomy (hoof, mane, withers, tail) + counting (4 hooves, 2 eyes, 1 tail). Introduce the word *gentle* and ask each kid to show you what gentle looks like with their own hands. |
| 0:15–0:25 | Read-aloud with Daisy nearby. Kids in a semicircle. Choose one of the Native-authored picture books from the suggested list. |
| 0:25–0:28 | Each kid gets a 20-second touch + a quiet "thank you" to Daisy |
| 0:28–0:30 | Goodbye. Hand out take-home one-pagers for parents. |

### Materials for Smart Start
Same as Hayden Canyon but ADD:
- Stickers (lots — each kid gets one) — a roll of pony stickers or Tribal Cowboy logo stickers
- A board book or two for the 2-year-olds (different from the picture books for older kids)
- More patience and energy — three back-to-back classroom rotations is exhausting. Bring water and a snack for yourself.

## What to Capture During EVERY Demo Visit

This is the most important section. Every visit you do is potential case-study material for the 4-week Sprint pitches. Capture it deliberately.

### Photos (no kid faces unless releases are signed)
- Wide shot of the room with Daisy in center
- Daisy + teacher (with teacher's permission)
- Daisy with materials in front of her (sight word cards, books)
- Empty setup before kids arrive
- Backs-of-heads shot of kids gathered around Daisy
- Detail shots: Daisy's hoof, mane, the sight word cards in use

### Video clips (30–60 seconds each)
- Audio of a kid asking Daisy a question (kid not on camera — just voice)
- Pan around the room showing the setup
- A teacher saying one sentence about how the visit went (if they agree)

### Notes — jot these in your phone or notepad during/after
- The single best thing a kid said
- The single best thing the teacher said
- The thing that surprised you (good or bad)
- The activity that worked best
- The activity that flopped
- How long it actually took vs. the plan
- Any questions parents asked at pickup

### Numbers
- Number of kids in attendance
- Number of teachers/staff present
- Number of classrooms visited (for Smart Start rotation)

### Contacts
- Teacher's full name, email, phone (for the testimonial follow-up)
- Director/principal's contact info
- Any other staff who showed interest in the 4-week Sprint

## The Preschool 4-Week Sprint Adaptation

Since you're pitching preschools for the 4-week Sprint, you need a version that fits 3–5 year olds. The Reading with Daisy (K–2) version doesn't quite work — sight words aren't developmentally right for preschoolers.

### Program name: "Daisy's Preschool Pals" (placeholder)

**Target ages:** 3 through pre-K (ages 3–5)
**Structure:** Same 4-week, 1-visit-per-week format
**Cost to preschool:** Free
**Cost to sponsor:** $1,500 per preschool (smaller because shorter visits + younger kids = less material)

### What's different from Reading with Daisy

| Element | Reading with Daisy (K–2) | Daisy's Preschool Pals (3–5) |
|---|---|---|
| Visit length | 45 min | 25 min |
| Focus | Sight words | Vocabulary + sensory + early concepts |
| Activities | Sight word relay | Anatomy naming, counting (1–10), color matching |
| Read-aloud | Picture book | Short rhyming board book |
| Pen-pal letters | Full-page text | Picture-heavy with a few simple words |
| Take-home booklet | 8-page Pony Passport | 4-page "Daisy Sticker Book" |
| Visit 4 | Field trip + Clydesdale | Same — Clydesdale visit + sticker book completion party |

### The 4 visits at preschool level

**Visit 1 — "Hello Daisy"**
- Meet Daisy. Name body parts: hoof, mane, tail, eye.
- Count Daisy's hooves (1, 2, 3, 4).
- Read a short rhyming book.
- Each kid gets a sticker book with Daisy on the cover.

**Visit 2 — "Daisy's Colors"**
- Color matching game. Daisy stands in the middle, kids hold up colored cards: brown like her mane, white like her star, pink like her tongue.
- Vocabulary: brown, white, pink, soft, gentle, kind.
- Read-aloud.
- New sticker for the sticker book.

**Visit 3 — "Daisy's Friends"**
- Talk about animal friendships. Show photos of Daisy with her real-life friends at the ranch (other ponies, a goat, a barn cat).
- Vocabulary: friend, share, kind, help.
- Kids draw a picture of their best friend (human or pet) in their sticker book.
- Read-aloud about friendship.
- New sticker.

**Visit 4 — "The Big Day"**
- Daisy AND Abby the Clydesdale visit. Preschoolers SCREAM with delight at Abby's size.
- Outdoor visit or field trip to the farm.
- Sticker book completion party.
- Each kid gets a printed photo magnet of Daisy.
- Take-home one-pager for parents.

### Pen-pal letters at preschool level

These are much simpler. Picture-heavy. Read aloud by the teacher.

**Letter 1 example:**

> Hi friends!
>
> I am Daisy. I am a pony. (picture of Daisy)
>
> You touched my mane. It is **soft**.
>
> I will come back and see you next week.
>
> I love you!
>
> — Daisy 🐴

That's it. Whole letter. Younger kids respond to repetition + pictures + emotional simplicity.
