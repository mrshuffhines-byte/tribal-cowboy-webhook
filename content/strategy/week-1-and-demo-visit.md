# Week 1 Lesson Plan + Demo Visit
## Tribal Cowboy Equine Reading Program — Preschool through 2nd Grade

**Audience:** Preschool, Pre-K, Kindergarten, 1st grade, 2nd grade (ages 3–8)
**Two delivery models in this doc:**
1. **Week 1 of the 4-Week Sprint** — first of four weekly visits to the same class
2. **Demo Visit** — single one-time visit, used to introduce the program to a school that hasn't booked a Sprint yet

**Pony branding:** Neutral. All written materials say "the pony" or "your pony friend." Stacie uses the pony's actual name in person at the visit, but no name appears on letters, certificates, or passports — that way the same materials work for any pony Stacie brings.

**Clydesdale:** Appears only at Visit 4 of the Sprint. Never at a demo visit.

## Part 1 — Week 1 of the 4-Week Sprint

### Two tracks, one Week 1

The same Week 1 runs in two versions depending on the age of the class.

| Track | Ages | Visit length | Lead activity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-K Track** | 3–5 (Preschool, Pre-K) | 25 min | Sensory + vocabulary + counting |
| **K-2 Track** | 5–8 (K, 1st, 2nd) | 45 min | Sight word relay + read-aloud |

If a school has both age groups, run both tracks back-to-back the same morning. The pony stays in one fixed location; classes rotate.

---

### Pre-Visit (the Monday before Visit 1)

Teacher reads the class a short "Welcome Letter" from the pony. Sets up anticipation.

> Hi friends!
>
> My name is a secret until Wednesday — that's when I'm coming to your school to meet you!
>
> I am a pony. My owner is named Stacie. We live at a place called Tribal Cowboy in a town called Athol, Idaho.
>
> When I come on Wednesday, please be ready to read with me. I love listening to kids read out loud.
>
> See you soon!
>
> — Your pony friend 🐴

### Pre-K Track — Week 1 (25 minutes, ages 3–5)

**Theme:** Hello, I'm a pony. You can be gentle with me.

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:03** | Arrival + introduction | Stacie introduces herself in one sentence: *"I am Stacie Hines. I am Nisenan Maidu. This is my pony, and the land we're on today is the traditional home of the Schitsu'umsh people."* Wave hello to the pony as a group. |
| **0:03–0:10** | Pony parts game | Point at the pony's parts and name them slowly: **hoof, mane, tail, eye, nose.** Kids repeat each word. Then count together: *"How many hooves? 1, 2, 3, 4. How many eyes? 1, 2. How many tails? 1."* |
| **0:10–0:18** | Read-aloud | One short picture book with big, clear images. Kids sit in a semicircle, pony stands nearby. Recommended: *Fry Bread* (Kevin Noble Maillard), *We Are Water Protectors* (Carole Lindstrom), or any short rhyming horse-themed board book. |
| **0:18–0:23** | Sensory touch (supervised) | One kid at a time, 20 seconds. Touch the pony's mane, then her shoulder. Repeat the vocabulary: **soft, warm, gentle.** Other kids watch from the circle. |
| **0:23–0:25** | Goodbye | Wave goodbye. Teacher hands each kid a sticker and the Pony Pals sticker book starter page. |

**No sight words at this age.** Developmentally inappropriate. Focus is vocabulary, counting, sensory.

---

### K-2 Track — Week 1 (45 minutes, ages 5–8)

**Theme:** Hello, I'm a pony. Hello, we're readers.

| Time | Activity | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:05** | Arrival + introduction | One-sentence land acknowledgment: *"I am Stacie Hines. I am Nisenan Maidu, and this is my pony. The land we're on today is the traditional home of the Schitsu'umsh people."* No lecture. |
| **0:05–0:15** | Pony anatomy + 4 vocab words | Point on the pony: **hoof, mane, withers, tail.** Kids touch their own body where the pony's body part would be (mane = hair, withers = shoulders). |
| **0:15–0:30** | Sight word relay | 5 laminated cards on the floor: **I, am, a, the, you.** Each kid takes a turn walking a few steps beside the pony (Stacie holds the lead), stops at a card, reads it aloud. The pony "delivers" the word. |
| **0:30–0:40** | Read-aloud | One Native-authored picture book. Class in a semicircle, pony nearby. Recommended titles: *Jingle Dancer* (Cynthia Leitich Smith), *Fry Bread* (Kevin Noble Maillard), *We Are Water Protectors* (Carole Lindstrom). |
| **0:40–0:45** | Q&A + goodbye | Hand out the Pony Passport booklet, one per kid. Tell them the pony will write them a letter this week. |

**Sight words this week:** I, am, a, the, you
**Idaho ELA standards hit:** K.RF.3.c, 1.RF.3.g (sight word fluency)

The teacher gives a quick before/after sight word check (read the 20 program words aloud to each kid privately, mark which ones they recognize). This is what we hand sponsors at the end of the Sprint.

## Part 2 — The Demo Visit

### What a demo visit is

A demo visit is a **single 25–45 minute classroom visit** that introduces a school to the Tribal Cowboy program. It is NOT part of a 4-week Sprint. It is what you offer a school that has never worked with you before.

The demo visit has two jobs:
1. Give the kids a great experience and the teacher a great photo of their classroom
2. Convert the school into a **4-Week Sprint customer** for the following semester

Every demo visit ends with a take-home one-pager that pitches the Sprint to parents and a follow-up email to the teacher that pitches the Sprint to the school.

### When to offer a demo

- A new school that asks "can you come visit?" but isn't ready to commit to four weeks
- A preschool director who wants to test the program before committing to a sponsor-funded Sprint
- A library or homeschool co-op that wants a one-time event
- An admin who needs to see it before they recommend it to their board

### What to charge for a demo

**For schools:** Free. The demo is your sales call.
**For private events (e.g. a homeschool co-op that won't be running a Sprint):** $250 flat for 45 minutes plus mileage outside North Idaho.

The only difference: at the end, hand the teacher the **Demo Visit Take-Home One-Pager** (template at the bottom of this doc) instead of the Pony Pals sticker book starter. The one-pager pitches the 4-Week Sprint to parents.

### Demo Visit Lesson Plan — K-2 Track (45 minutes, ages 5–8)

Same as Sprint Week 1 K-2 Track above, with one swap:

- Instead of handing out the full Pony Passport (which is meant to be used across 4 weeks), hand out a **Souvenir Mini-Passport** — a single folded page with the cover, a "I met a pony on [DATE]" page, and the Demo Visit Take-Home One-Pager bound in.
- Mention to the kids: *"If your teacher decides we should come back four more times, you'll get a real Pony Passport next time."*

That single line turns the kids into Sprint advocates. They'll bug their teacher for the Sprint.

### Demo Visit Run-of-Show (logistics)

Same logistics regardless of age track.

**Day before**
- Confirm everything by email with the teacher: arrival time, room location, group size, ages, any allergies, photo release status
- Wash the pony, trim hooves if needed
- Pack the truck

**Morning of**
- Arrive 30 minutes early
- Set up in one fixed location (classroom or gym) — pony stays put, kids come in
- Walk the pony around the empty room so she gets used to it before kids arrive
- Greet the teacher by name, hand her a small framed pony photo as a thank-you gift ($15 from any drugstore — biggest goodwill purchase you'll ever make)

**Run the visit** (Pre-K or K-2 plan above)

**Before you leave**
- Hand the teacher the **4-Week Sprint One-Pager** in person
- Get the teacher's full name, email, phone for the follow-up
- Ask: *"Would you mind sharing one sentence about how the visit went? Even something short."* — this is the testimonial you'll use to pitch the next school

**Within 2 hours of leaving** (do this from the truck in the parking lot or first rest stop)
- Batch the photos through the Tribal Cowboy overlay template (school name + date already filled in)
- Upload to a single class gallery titled: *Mrs. [Teacher Name]'s Class — [School] — [Date]*
- Generate one QR code that links to the gallery
- Email the teacher with the QR code and a short paragraph she can email home to parents

**Within 24 hours**
- Send the post-visit follow-up email (template below) — this is the email that turns a demo into a Sprint

---

### Pony Welfare Notes (for any visit)

Watch for these signs and pause the visit if you see them:

- Tail swishing more than usual
- Pinned ears
- Lifting feet anxiously
- Refusing treats
- Heavy sweating in a cool room
- Sudden head-tossing or backing up

Take a 30-minute quiet break, water, hay. The school will understand. Pick the pony who LOVES kids and stays calm under crowd noise — not the easily-spooked one.

## Capture Plan (apply to every visit)

The single most important habit. Every visit you do is potential case-study material for the next pitch. Capture deliberately.

**Photos** (no kid faces unless releases are signed):
- Wide shot of the room with the pony in center
- Pony + teacher (with teacher's permission)
- Pony with materials in front of her (sight word cards, books)
- Empty room setup before kids arrive
- Backs-of-heads shot of kids gathered around the pony
- Detail shots: hoof, mane, sight word cards in use

**Video clips** (30–60 seconds each):
- Audio of a kid asking the pony a question (kid not on camera — just voice)
- A teacher saying one sentence about how the visit went (if they agree)

**Notes** (jot these in the truck right after):
- The single best thing a kid said
- The single best thing the teacher said
- The thing that surprised you
- The activity that worked best
- The activity that flopped or felt rushed
- How long it actually took vs. the plan

**Numbers:**
- Total kids
- Total teachers/staff present
- Number of classrooms or groups

**Contacts:**
- Teacher's full name, email, phone
- Director/principal's contact info
- Any other staff who showed interest in the Sprint

## Final Note

The Week 1 visit and the Demo Visit are almost the same lesson — that's the point. The Demo Visit is Week 1 in disguise, used to win a Sprint contract. If a school books a Sprint after a demo, the kids already had Week 1 — so Sprint Visit 1 becomes a recap-and-build session, with the same sight words tightened.

The take-home one-pager and the follow-up email are doing the real selling. Treat them like sales tools, not afterthoughts. One signed Sprint pays for 8 demo visits.
