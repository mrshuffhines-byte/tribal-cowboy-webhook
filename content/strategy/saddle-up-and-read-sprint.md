# Saddle Up & Read — The 4-Week Reading Sprint
## Full Program Specification — Preschool through 2nd Grade

**Program:** Saddle Up & Read
**Target ages:** Preschool, Pre-K, Kindergarten, 1st grade, 2nd grade (ages 3–8)
**Two age tracks:** Pre-K Track (ages 3–5) + K-2 Track (ages 5–8)
**Duration:** 4 weekly visits + 3 between-visit pen-pal letters + 1 final Clydesdale reveal
**Pony branding:** Neutral throughout. All written materials say "the pony" or "your pony friend." Stacie uses the real pony's name at the visit. This way the same printed materials work no matter which pony is brought on any given day.
**Clydesdale:** Visit 4 only. Abby the Clydesdale appears outdoors or at the farm as the BIG REVEAL after three weeks of pen-pal buildup. She is the only animal named in the program.
**Cost to school:** Free
**Cost to sponsor:** $2,000 per K-2 Sprint, $1,500 per Pre-K Sprint
**Designed to serve:** Neurotypical kids AND kids with ADHD, sensory processing differences, anxiety, and emerging readers — by design, not by accommodation.

## What Makes This Program ADHD-Friendly by Design

Every visit follows the same predictable structure because ADHD brains thrive on predictability. Every activity is short — under 7 minutes for Pre-K, under 10 for K-2 — because sustained attention isn't the goal here, **engaged attention is.** Every block has built-in movement, sensory input, and choice. Every visit has the same opening ritual, the same closing ritual, and the same "reset moment" — the **Pony Breath** — that the kids can use forever after the program is over.

This isn't an ADHD adaptation bolted onto a regular program. The whole program is designed around the way real K-2 brains actually pay attention.

### The 9 ADHD-Friendly Design Principles

1. **Short, varied blocks.** No activity longer than 7 min (Pre-K) or 10 min (K-2). The visit moves.
2. **Visual schedule the kids can see.** Laminated picture cards on a felt board show what's coming next. Stacie moves a clip down the board as the visit progresses. Kids always know where they are in the visit.
3. **Predictable opening + closing ritual.** Every visit starts and ends the same way. Anchors a chaotic week.
4. **Movement embedded in learning.** Sight word relay = kids walk with the pony. Read-aloud = kids do body motions. Counting = kids stomp or clap.
5. **The Pony Breath reset.** Whenever energy spikes or focus drops, Stacie says "Pony Breath" — everyone breathes slowly in sync with the pony for 30 seconds. This is co-regulation, taught with a real living metronome.
6. **Choice within structure.** Kids pick which sight word card to grab, which mat to sit on, which book page to look at next. Choice reduces resistance.
7. **Parallel stations for big classes.** No kid waits more than 90 seconds for a turn. For classes over 15, run two pony-side stations and rotate.
8. **Multi-sensory everything.** Touch (mane, hay), smell (pony, hay), sound (hooves, breath), sight (cards, books), proprioception (walking, stomping). Every kid finds at least one channel that works for them.
9. **Spot markers.** Every kid has a designated hay-bale square or yoga mat as their visual home base. Removes the "where do I go?" overwhelm.

### What This Means in Practice

| Standard classroom challenge | What Saddle Up & Read does instead |
|---|---|
| "Sit still and listen for 20 minutes" | 7-minute max blocks, all involving movement or sensory input |
| "Don't fidget" | Every kid is touching hay, holding a card, walking with the pony, or stomping a count — fidgeting *is* the lesson |
| "Wait your turn" | Parallel stations + active waiting (count the pony's spots, find the next sight word) |
| "Quiet voice, please" | Quiet voices are taught as a tradition of approaching animals — not a behavior rule |
| "Pay attention to me" | Attention is split across pony, schedule board, sensory input — the pony does half the work |
| "Hands to yourself" | Hands are ON something every minute — the pony, hay, a card, a sticker, a passport |

### Why the Pony Matters Most

Animal-assisted regulation is well-documented for ADHD. The pony provides:
- A slow, steady breath rhythm kids can match (co-regulation)
- Warm sensory input (touching her coat)
- A non-judging audience (which is why reading-aloud-to-pony works for anxious readers)
- A reason to use a quiet body (without being told to)
- A focal point that pulls scattered attention back into one room

The pony does not need to be trained to do tricks. She needs to **stand calmly and accept gentle contact.** That's it. The pony's calmness is the lesson.

## The Pony Breath Ritual

Every visit, at least twice. Used as a reset whenever energy spikes.

Stacie says: *"Pony Breath."*

Kids: place a hand on their belly. Look at the pony. Match the pony's breath. One slow breath in. One slow breath out. Three rounds. Quiet.

This takes 30–45 seconds. It works because it's a real practice tied to a real animal, not a "calm down" instruction.

**Teach it explicitly at Visit 1.** Reinforce at every visit. By Visit 4, kids do it on their own.

This is the centerpiece visit. Plan extra carefully.

Or: half the class reads while half does a quiet sight word puzzle, then swap. |
| **0:38–0:42** | The pony's question | Stacie speaks "as" the pony: *"Did anyone hear a word they didn't know?"* Group sounds it out together. |

| **0:44–0:45** | Goodbye | |

### Pro tip for Visit 3

If you let the read-aloud run, it will eat the whole visit. Set a quiet timer (a small bell, not a buzzer). Each kid gets the same beat. Time-bounded fairness is critical for ADHD kids who can fixate on "did she get longer than me?"

## The Demo Visit

### What a demo visit is

A demo visit is a **single 25–45 minute classroom visit** that introduces a school to the program. It is NOT part of a 4-week Sprint. Use it to land a school that has never worked with Tribal Cowboy before.

The demo visit has two jobs:
1. Give the kids a great experience and the teacher a great photo of their classroom
2. Convert the school into a **4-Week Sprint customer** for the following semester

### When to offer a demo

- A new school that asks "can you come visit?" but isn't ready for four weeks
- A preschool director who wants to test before committing to a sponsor-funded Sprint
- A library, homeschool co-op, or microschool wanting a one-time event
- An admin who needs to see it in person before recommending it to their board

### What to charge for a demo

- **For schools (potential Sprint customers):** Free. The demo is your sales call.
- **For private events that won't run a Sprint (e.g., a homeschool co-op event):** $250 flat for 45 minutes plus mileage outside North Idaho.

### Demo Visit Lesson Plan

**Use the Visit 1 lesson plan from above** — Pre-K Track or K-2 Track depending on the school. Same flow, same Pony Breath, same sight words (K-2 only), same visual schedule.

Three small differences from Sprint Visit 1:

1. **Hand out a Souvenir Mini-Passport** (single folded page) instead of the full Pony Passport. Tell the kids: *"If your teacher decides we should come back four more times, you'll get a real Pony Passport next time."* That single line turns the kids into Sprint advocates.

2. **Hand the teacher the 4-Week Sprint One-Pager in person before you leave.** Don't leave it on a desk. Hand it.

3. **Send the post-visit follow-up email within 24 hours** (template below). This is the email that turns a demo into a Sprint.

### Demo Visit Logistics

**Day before**
- Confirm everything by email with the teacher: arrival time, room location, group size, ages, allergies, photo release status
- Wash the pony, trim hooves if needed
- Pack the truck

**Morning of**
- Arrive 30 min early
- Set up in one fixed location — pony stays put, kids come in
- Walk the pony around the empty room before kids arrive
- Greet the teacher by name, hand her a small framed pony photo ($15 from any drugstore — biggest goodwill purchase you'll ever make)

**Run the visit** (Pre-K or K-2 Visit 1 plan)

**Before you leave**
- Hand the teacher the 4-Week Sprint One-Pager
- Get her full name, email, phone
- Ask: *"Would you mind sharing one sentence about how the visit went?"*

**Within 2 hours of leaving** (from the truck, in the parking lot)
- Batch the photos through the Tribal Cowboy overlay template
- Upload to a single class gallery titled: *Mrs. [Teacher Name]'s Class — [School] — [Date]*
- Generate one QR code that links to the gallery
- Email the teacher with the QR code and a paragraph she can forward to parents

## ADHD Accommodation Notes for Teachers

Share this with classroom teachers a week before Visit 1. Most of what they need is already built into the program — but a few extras help.

**Before the visit:**
- If you know a student typically has trouble with new people or animals, let me know — I'll plan a softer first interaction for them
- Send the Welcome Letter home so parents can talk about it with their child
- Have a "quiet corner" available in the room — a spot a kid can move to if they need a break. They will not be excluded from the activity. They will rejoin when ready.

**During the visit:**
- It's fine for a student to look at the schedule board mid-activity. Don't redirect.
- It's fine for a student to choose not to touch the pony. They participate by watching.
- The Pony Breath is the universal opt-out — any kid can call for one at any moment.

**After the visit:**
- The Pony Breath is theirs to keep. Ask kids to show YOU how to do it for the rest of the school year.
- The pen-pal letter arrives Monday. Reading it aloud as a class is part of the program.

## Pony Welfare Protocol

A pony doing 4–6 sessions in a week is a working pony. Watch for stress signals and pause if you see them:

- Tail swishing more than usual
- Pinned ears
- Lifting feet anxiously
- Refusing treats
- Heavy sweating in a cool room
- Sudden head-tossing or backing up

If you see any of these:
- Cut the session short, give her 30 min in a quiet space, water, hay
- Let the school know you're prioritizing the pony's well-being
- Reschedule whatever is left

**Pick the RIGHT pony for school work.** The one who LOVES kids and crowds and stays calm under pressure. Not the easily-spooked one. If you're not 100% sure, take the most chill horse you have.

**Required between visits:** 24+ hours of recovery time before another school visit. The pony is a worker, not a machine.

## Final Note

The program works because **four things happen at the same time:**

1. **Kids learn** — sight words or vocabulary, cultural context, a regulation tool (Pony Breath)
2. **Kids feel** — the pony is a friend, the letters are real, the program is hers and his and theirs
3. **ADHD brains regulate** — short blocks, movement, sensory input, predictability, Pony Breath
4. **The program is sellable** — clean four-week unit, measurable outcomes, $1,500–$2,000 per sponsor, photo-rich case studies

The Pony Breath is the secret weapon. It costs nothing to teach. It works for every kid, ADHD or not. By Visit 4, you'll watch a class do it on their own when energy spikes — without a single adult saying anything. That moment is what tells you the program works.

The letters are the second secret weapon. They cost nothing to make and they multiply the emotional bond by ten. The first time a teacher hands a letter from the pony to a class and watches the kids' faces light up, you'll know.
