### Tribal Cowboy LLC — Athol, Idaho

**What this doc is:** The working operational run sheet for Pre-K Visit 1. Same script and timing whether it's Visit 1 of a full Sprint or a standalone demo. Print this. Read it on the drive over. Hold it in your hand at the school.

# Part 2 — The Morning Of

## T-3 hours — Pony Prep (at the ranch)

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| T-3:00 | Feed her — light meal. She's a working pony today, not a horse on a 9-hour drive. |
| T-2:50 | Walk her around the pasture. Loosens her up. |
| T-2:40 | Brush — full grooming. Pay special attention to her mane (kids will touch it) and her tail (kids will look at it). |
| T-2:20 | Pick out hooves. Check for stones or thrush. |
| T-2:10 | Bathroom break — let her relieve herself before loading. Walk her to her usual spot. |
| T-2:00 | Load her in the trailer. |

**Costume note:** DON'T put the costume on her at the ranch. Mailbag and bandana go on at the school after she's unloaded. She doesn't trailer in costume.

## T-1:30 — Drive

Allow extra time. North Idaho weather is unpredictable in the school year (Sept–May). Snow, ice, fog all add time. Plan to arrive 30–45 min early no matter what.

## T-30 min — Arrive at School

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Park** | Visitor lot if available. Otherwise the closest spot to your unload entrance. NEVER block a fire lane. |
| **Walk in alone** | Leave the pony in the trailer with hay net + water for 15–20 min while you set up. |
| **Find the teacher** | Front office first. Sign in. Get a visitor badge. |
| **Hand the teacher the framed photo** | DO IT NOW, not at the end. Sets the tone. |
| **Walk to the room** | Note the route — you'll walk the pony this way in 15 min. Look for slippery floors, sharp turns, low-hanging signs that might spook her. |

## T-20 min — Set Up the Classroom

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Decide pony's spot** | Center of the room, away from windows (no startle), back to a wall if possible. |
| **Lay the rubber mat** | 8'×8' under where the pony will stand. School floors are slippery. |
| **Place spot markers** | 15 yoga squares in a semicircle 4–6 feet from the pony's mat |
| **Set up visual schedule** | Felt board on a chair or easel where ALL kids can see |
| **Place the book** | On a small table near where you'll stand |
| **Pre-position stickers** | In one stack, ready to hand to teacher at minute 22 |
| **Sticker books** | Stack at the entrance — teacher hands them out as kids enter |
| **Check the room** | Anything dangling (string, ribbon, decorations) that could spook a pony? Anything reflective that could catch her eye? Move what you can. |

## T-5 min — Bring in the Pony

| Action | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Walk her in slowly** | One adult per side if possible. Quiet voice. |
| **Let her sniff** | The doorway, the floor, the room. 30 seconds of sniffing before you ask anything else. |
| **Costume on** | Mailbag attached to her saddle/girth. Red bandana around her neck. Quick check that nothing dangles or pulls. |
| **Position her on the mat** | Halter and lead in your hand or looped on a stable post / chair. NEVER tie her to something she could pull over. |
| **Let her stand** | 60 seconds to settle before kids enter. |

# Part 3 — THE 25-MINUTE VISIT

This is the show. Hold this page in your hand.

## 0:00–0:03 — HELLO + Visual Schedule (3 min)

**You say:**
> *"Hi everybody! I'm Stacie. I'm a horse person from a place called Tribal Cowboy in Athol, Idaho. And this is my pony, [her name].*
>
> *Today she has a special job. Can you see her mail bag? She's wearing it because she's a Pony Express Rider. That means she delivers letters and books!*
>
> *Before we do anything else, I want to show you our schedule. Look at this board. We're going to do six things today. First — say hello. Then we'll learn her body. Then a special breath. Then a story. Then you can touch her. Then we say bye."*

**You do:**
- Stand beside the pony, slightly forward of her shoulder
- Hold the visual schedule board at kid height
- Point at each picture card as you name it
- Move the clip to the first card (👋 Hello)

**Pony does:**
- Stands calmly on the mat
- Mailbag visible

**Kids do:**
- Sit on their spot markers
- Watch you

**Backup:** If kids are over-excited and won't settle, skip the schedule explanation and jump straight to **Whoa, partners! / Pony Ears** (see below). Calm first, content second.

**Also during these first 3 minutes — teach the two callbacks:**

> *"Before we start, I want to teach you TWO things. Watch me.*
>
> *FIRST — when I say 'SADDLE UP!' — you say 'GIDDY UP!' and you stomp your feet three times like a pony pawing the ground, AND you make your PONY HOOVES like this. (Demonstrate: curl your fingers into fists, knuckles up, hold both hands close to your tummy.) That tells me you're ready to RIDE.*
>
> *Let's try it. SADDLE UP!"*
>
> Kids: *"GIDDY UP!"* + stomp 3 times + Pony Hooves at tummy.
>
> *"Yee-haw! That was great.*
>
> *SECOND — when I say 'WHOA, PARTNERS!' — you FREEZE, and you put on your PONY EARS like this. (Demonstrate: cup both hands behind your ears.) That tells me you're ready to LISTEN, just like a pony listens.*
>
> *Let's try it. WHOA, PARTNERS!"*
>
> Kids freeze + cup hands behind ears.
>
> *"Perfect. Saddle Up for go. Whoa Partners for listen. We'll use these all morning."*

This costs about 60 seconds. Pays back the whole rest of the visit.

---

## 0:03–0:06 — The 3 Big Rules (3 min)

**You say:**
> *"Before anybody touches a pony, we have to know THREE rules. Just three. Hold up three fingers like me.*
>
> *RULE ONE: **Inside voice.** No screaming. Ponies don't like big loud noises. They think a scream means something is hunting them. Say 'inside voice.'"*
>
> **Kids:** *"Inside voice."*
>
> *"RULE TWO: **Walk, don't run.** Quick moving things scare a pony. Walk slow like you're sneaking up on a deer. Say 'walk, don't run.'"*
>
> **Kids:** *"Walk, don't run."*
>
> *"RULE THREE: **Always tell the pony you're there.** Especially if you walk near her back. Just say 'Hi, pony.' I'll show you why in a minute. Say 'tell the pony.'"*
>
> **Kids:** *"Tell the pony."*
>
> *"Three rules. Hold up three fingers. Inside voice. Walk slow. Tell the pony. Got it?"*

**You do:**
- Hold up three fingers as you say each rule
- Match the kids' eyes when they repeat back
- Don't go fast — let them hear each rule land

**Kids do:**
- Hold up three fingers
- Say each rule back

**Backup:** If kids race ahead and say all 3 at once, slow them down — "One at a time. One. Two. Three."

---

## 0:06–0:12 — Pony Vision (6 min)

This is the lesson kids tell their parents at dinner.

**You say:**
> *"Watch this. I'm going to show you how the pony sees the world.*
>
> *Cover your eyes with your hands. (Pause.) Now uncover them. Look STRAIGHT ahead. Can you see your friend next to you without turning your head? A little bit, right?*
>
> *That's because YOUR eyes are on the FRONT of your face. You see straight ahead.*
>
> *Now look at the pony. Where are her eyes? On the SIDE of her head. One here. One here. (Point.) That means she can see ALMOST ALL THE WAY AROUND her body. Like she has eyes in the back of her head.*
>
> *BUT — there are TWO spots she CAN'T see.*
>
> *Number ONE: right in front of her nose. (Point.) That's a blind spot.*
>
> *Number TWO: right behind her tail. (Point.) THAT'S the most important one.*
>
> *Watch — I'm going to walk around her slowly. Tell me when she can see me and when she can't."*

**You do:**
- Walk slowly around the pony in a wide circle, narrating
- *"Now she can see me. (Side.) Now she can see me. (Other side.) Now I'm at her face — close blind spot. Now I'm BEHIND her — she CAN'T SEE ME. Hi, pony!"* (Speak so the pony hears you.)
- Return to the front.

**You then say:**
> *"That's why our third rule matters. If you walk behind a pony and don't tell her you're there, she might get SCARED. And a scared pony might KICK — not because she's mean, but because she got surprised. So we always say 'Hi, pony' so she knows where we are."*

**Pony does:**
- Stands calmly
- May turn her head to follow you — totally fine

**Kids do:**
- Watch
- Some will narrate with you — "now she can see you!"

**Backup:** If kids are restless, shorten the demonstration to just the "blind spot behind her tail" moment. That's the single most important takeaway.

---

## 0:12–0:17 — Pony Looks + Synonyms (5 min)

The body language lesson. Use flip cards or act each one out yourself with the pony as backdrop.

**You say:**
> *"Now we're going to learn how the pony TALKS to us with her body. She doesn't have words. So she uses her ears, her eyes, and her tail.*
>
> *I'm going to show you SIX things. For each one, you say it back with me. AND we'll learn another word that means the same thing."*

**Hold up Card 1 — Ears forward:**
> *"Look at her ears. Pointed forward. That's HAPPY ears. She's curious. Another word: INTERESTED.*
>
> *Say it with me: HAPPY EARS. INTERESTED."*

**Kids:** *"HAPPY EARS. INTERESTED."*

**Card 2 — Ears pinned back:**
> *"Ears flat against her head. That's GRUMPY ears. She's saying 'leave me alone.' Another word: MAD.*
>
> *Say it: GRUMPY EARS. MAD."*

**Kids:** *"GRUMPY EARS. MAD."*

**Card 3 — Soft eyes:**
> *"Soft, blinking eyes. That's CALM eyes. Another word: RELAXED.*
>
> *Say it: CALM EYES. RELAXED."*

**Card 4 — Wide eyes with white showing:**
> *"Wide eyes, you can see the white part — that's SCARED eyes. Another word: AFRAID.*
>
> *Say it: SCARED EYES. AFRAID."*

**Card 5 — Still tail:**
> *"Tail not moving — that's QUIET tail. Another word: CALM.*
>
> *Say it: QUIET TAIL. CALM."*

**Card 6 — Tail swishing:**
> *"Tail going fast like a fly is bugging her — that's MAD tail. Another word: ANNOYED.*
>
> *Say it: MAD TAIL. ANNOYED."*

**Then the 3 Warning Signs:**
> *"Last thing. There are THREE signs that tell you a pony is going to bite or kick. If you see any of these, you STOP and tell a grown-up. Ready?*
>
> *Sign one — ears FLAT against her head.*
> *Sign two — tail SWISHING hard and fast.*
> *Sign three — lifting one foot OFF the ground.*
>
> *A pony only has a few ways to say no. She can swat with her tail. She can bite. She can kick. All three can hurt. So we WATCH her body. We LISTEN. Before she has to tell us in the scary ways."*

**You do:**
- Hold up each flip card OR act the look out yourself (eyes wide, ears mimed flat, etc.)
- Pause for kids to repeat each pair
- Slow down for the 3 Warning Signs — these matter most

**Kids do:**
- Repeat each word + synonym
- Hold up imaginary "ears" with their hands for the ear positions

**Backup:** If a kid struggles with the synonyms, just have them repeat the main word. The synonym is the bonus, not the requirement.

---

## 0:17–0:21 — Read-Aloud: *Tribal Cowboy Pony, What Do You See?* (4 min)

**You say:**
> *"I have a book for us. This is MY book — I made it. It's called Tribal Cowboy Pony, What Do You See? And every animal in the book is a real animal from my ranch. Watch."*

**Read the book. The repeating pattern lets kids predict the next page.**

By spread 3, kids will start saying *"what do you see?"* with you. Let them.

**You do:**
- Hold the oversized book at kid height
- Show every photo
- Turn pages at a steady pace
- Pause on the Clydesdale spread — let them GASP
- Land the last spread (*"What do YOU see?"*) and look around the room

**Pony does:**
- Stands near you, relaxed

**Kids do:**
- Listen
- Look at the photos
- Chant the repeating question with you

**Backup:** If kids are restless by spread 5, skip to the Clydesdale spread and the closer. Better to cut early than fight a lost room.

---

## 0:21–0:24 — Sensory Touch — Approach From the Side (3 min)

**You say:**
> *"Now you each get a turn to touch the pony. Remember our rules. Remember her blind spots. Walk SLOW. Stay to her SIDE — never behind her. Touch her mane. Say SOFT. Walk back.*
>
> *If you don't want to touch her, that's okay. You can stay on your spot. You're still part of the group."*

**You do:**
- Stand beside the pony, hand on the halter
- Demonstrate the side-approach yourself first — slow, gentle, mane then shoulder
- Call each kid by name
- Reinforce the rule: *"Approach from the side. That's right."*
- About 12–15 seconds per kid

**Pony does:**
- Stands still
- Accepts touch from the side

**Kids do:**
- One at a time
- Walk slowly UP THE SIDE (not behind)
- Touch mane, touch shoulder, say "soft"
- Walk back

**Backup:** If a kid is scared, offer "watch from your spot." If the pony shows any warning sign (ears flat, tail swishing fast, foot lifted), pause the line. Have the class identify the body language — *"What is her tail saying? MAD. So we give her a minute."* Reinforces the lesson in real-time.

Kids call out the answer (probably "happy ears!").

**You say:**
> *"Good readers. You can read a pony already. Take a sticker from your teacher and put it on your Pony Pals book."*

Stickers distributed.

**You say:**

>
> *Watch your mailbox. The pony is going to write you a letter. A real letter, with a real stamp. Your teacher will read it to you.*
>
> *Wave bye to the pony!"*

**You do:**

**Pony does:**

**Kids do:**
- Wave

# Part 6 — Backup Plans for Common Edge Cases

## Pony has an off day

**Signs:** Won't load, anxious in trailer, refuses to walk into the building, refuses treats.

**What to do:** Don't push it. Call the teacher from the parking lot. Reschedule for next week. *"My pony is having an off morning — I'd rather come back than push her through a stressful visit. Can we move to [date]?"* Most teachers will understand. Some will even appreciate the boundary.

## Weather is bad

**Snow/ice:** Trailer goes slow. Leave 30 min earlier. If roads are closed, call to reschedule.

**Heavy rain:** Bring a tarp for the pony at the door. Wipe her down before she goes in. Bring extra paper towels for the floor.

**Hot day (over 80°F):** Limit transit time. Cool her down with water at the trailer between visits. Cut the visit to 20 min if needed.

## More kids than expected

**Plan for 15, walk in to find 25:** Split into two groups, run the same 25-min program twice back-to-back. The pony gets a 5-min break between. Total day adds 30 min. Bring extra stickers.

## Fewer kids than expected

**Plan for 15, find 6:** Same program, just more touch time per kid. Stretch the visit to 30 min if the energy is good. Better to over-deliver on a small group than rush.

## A kid is terrified

**Signs:** Hiding behind the teacher, crying, won't sit on a spot marker.

**What to do:** Have the teacher sit with them at the back of the room. Offer them a "watcher" job — they hold the visual schedule clip and move it for you between activities. They're still part of the group. By Visit 2, most will move closer.

## A kid is allergic

**Signs:** Sneezing, watery eyes, red rash.

**What to do:** Move them to a spot 10+ feet away. Don't let them touch the pony. Have the teacher take them out of the room if symptoms escalate. Apologize, debrief with teacher after.

## The pony pees/poops in the room

**It happens.** It will happen at least once in your first 10 visits.

**What to do:** Don't panic. Don't apologize too much — kids will mirror your energy. Calmly say *"Yep, that happens — she's a pony!"* Pull out the muck bucket + gloves + paper towels. Clean it up while talking to the class. Make it a teaching moment: *"This is what happens when a real animal is in your room. We clean up after our animals."* The teacher will laugh. The kids will tell the story for years.

## The schedule runs short

**You're done at minute 18 and you've still got 7 min:** Do another round of body language flip cards. Let kids ask the pony questions (Stacie answers as the pony). Add a second round of sensory touch. Re-read 1 page of the book. Never panic-improvise something unrehearsed.

## The schedule runs long

**You're at minute 27 and not done:** Skip the second sensory touch round. Go straight to sticker + body language check + goodbye. Get out clean. The TEACHER's schedule matters more than yours.

## The teacher cancels last minute

**Morning-of cancellation:** Confirm in writing. Reschedule. DON'T drive over hoping it's a miscommunication. Your pony has nothing to gain from a wasted day.

# Final Note

The first visit is the hardest. By Visit 2, the kids know what to expect, the pony knows the room, and you have a rhythm. The first visit is where you find out if your pony is a "school pony" or not — if she paws nervously, swishes her tail constantly, or won't accept touch, this isn't her job. There's no shame in pivoting to a different pony for the rest of the Sprint.

But if she stands calmly, accepts touch, breathes slow, and lets a kid pet her mane — you've got it. Everything else is just repetition.

Now go. The kids are waiting.
