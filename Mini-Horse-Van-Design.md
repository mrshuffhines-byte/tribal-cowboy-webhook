# Tribal Cowboy Mini Horse Van — Design Spec

**Purpose:** Haul two miniature horses to schools, photo sessions, parties, and corporate events around North Idaho and Eastern Washington without smelling like a barn when you park it.

**Date:** 2026-05-19
**Owner:** Stacie / Tribal Cowboy LLC

---

## 1. The Vehicle

**Ford Transit T-350HD Extended High Roof — single rear wheel**
Built in Claycomo, Missouri (Kansas City Assembly Plant). USA-made, USA-serviced, dealer support in Coeur d'Alene and Spokane.

| Spec | Number |
|---|---|
| Interior cargo length | 12 ft 8 in (152") |
| Interior cargo width (between wheel wells) | 5 ft 10 in (70") |
| Interior cargo height | 6 ft 9 in (81.5") — you can stand up |
| GVWR | 10,360 lbs |
| Payload (after build-out) | ~2,000 lbs — plenty for two minis + tack + water |
| Engine | 3.5L V6 (gas) or 3.5L EcoBoost twin-turbo |
| MPG (loaded) | ~16–18 hwy |

**Why not the alternatives:**

- *Ram ProMaster* — built in Mexico. Not USA-made.
- *Chevy Express / Ford E-Series cutaway with box* — proven, but lower interior height and worse fuel economy. Older platform.
- *F-450 cab-and-chassis with custom box* — overkill for two minis. Better for hauling Millie or Abby (full-size Clydesdale).
- *Pull-behind trailer* — already have access. The point of this van is it goes places a trailer can't: schools with no truck pull-up, small downtown event lots, narrow ranch driveways.

**Used vs. new:** A 2022–2024 with <60K miles runs $35–45K. New 2026 runs $58–65K. Used is the right call — the conversion is the value, not the chassis.

## 3. How It Gets Fabricated

### Floor — the most important system

This is what makes or breaks the smell problem. Five layers, bottom to top:

1. **Factory steel floor** — every seam sealed with marine-grade urethane. No corrosion path.
2. **3/4" marine plywood subfloor** — sealed in two-part epoxy on all six sides before install. Wood never sees moisture.
3. **18-gauge 304 stainless steel pan** — rolled 4" up the walls. This is a literal waterproof tub. You could fill it with a garden hose and it would hold.
4. **1/2" perforated rubber stall mats** — sit on 1/4" plastic feet so liquid runs under, not into.
5. **Two 2" stainless drains** — one per stall, plumbed into a 5-gallon polyethylene catch tank mounted under the chassis (same gear RVs use for gray water). Empty at home via garden hose connection.

### Walls

- Closed-cell foam insulation between factory steel and inner skin (sound + temperature)
- Lower 48": 1" closed-cell foam covered in marine vinyl — padded kick wall, wipes clean
- Above 48" to roof: **Line-X spray-on bedliner** — non-porous, antimicrobial, won't hold smell or bacteria. Looks like a clean truck bed liner.

### Stalls

Two side-by-side rear-load stalls, separated by a **removable padded divider** on tongue-and-groove tracks. Pull the divider when you only need to haul one mini and want the extra room.

- 35" wide × 60" long each — sized for a 38" mini with room
- Padded chest bar + butt bar (1.5" aluminum, vinyl-wrapped foam)
- Rubber-lined manger at front of each stall, hay net hooks above
- Heavy rubber matting on every vertical surface a mini can kick

### Rear ramp

7-ft drop ramp with spring-assist (counterweighted — one hand opens it). Rubber tread, side curtains that roll out to extend a "tunnel" for skittish loaders. Folds flush, two stainless latches.

### Side ramp (passenger side)

5-ft folding ramp behind the side cargo door. This is the secret weapon for events: pull one mini out for a photo without disturbing the other. Load from a curb at schools where there's no back access.

### Windows

Replace factory side glass with **drop-down barred horse trailer windows** (Hayman or Lakota replacement panels). Mesh screen + bar guards. Light gets in, head can't get out.

### Tack & water room (front 24")

- Aluminum diamond plate floor
- 20-gallon fresh water tank with hand-pump spigot
- 5-gallon gray water tank
- Slide-out grooming kit drawer
- Saddle rack, halter hooks, lead rope coils
- Small fold-down work surface for paperwork at events

### Electrical

- House battery: Group 31 AGM, 100 Ah (separate from chassis)
- Battery isolator off the alternator — charges while you drive
- **200W solar panel** on roof — keeps the fans running all day at an event without idling the engine
- LED interior lights, 3000K warm (gentler on horses)
- 12V outlets for fans, future cameras
- 110V inverter for the ozone generator (post-trip only)

### Cameras

Two interior IR cameras with a 7" monitor on the dash. You watch the minis the whole drive. One backup camera at the rear bumper.

## 4. The Smell Problem — Solved

Horse vans get bad fast. Ammonia from urine, manure, wet hay, sweat — it lives in fabric and porous wood and never comes out. Most rigs are just barns on wheels. This one isn't.

**Ten things working together:**

### 1. No porous surfaces inside
Stainless floor pan, vinyl padding, Line-X walls. No carpet, no upholstery, no headliner, no exposed wood. Anywhere a smell can hide, it will — so nothing in this van hides anything.

### 2. Below-floor manure containment
Perforated mats over a stainless drain pan over a sealed catch tank. Pee never sits next to the horse. After unloading, you scoop solids and the rest is already gone.

### 3. Two-stage active ventilation with charcoal exhaust
Front roof fan **pulls fresh air in** over the minis. Rear roof fan **pushes used air out through a 4" inline activated charcoal canister** before it ever leaves the vehicle. (Same gear hydroponic grow rooms use to kill plant odor.) Result: at an event with the van parked behind a school, **nobody outside the van smells anything.** Both fans run on solar — no engine idle.

### 4. Pelleted pine bedding (not straw, not shavings)
Pellets absorb 4× their weight in liquid, lock in ammonia, don't break down into dust. One scoop per stall per trip. Compostable on the way out. Scoop is faster than shoveling shavings.

### 5. Post-trip ozone cycle
Mount a 3,500 mg/hr commercial ozone generator in the tack compartment. After unloading and rinsing, close the rig and run the cycle for 30 minutes, then auto-vent for 15. Ozone kills smell at the molecular level — not masking, actually breaking down the molecules. **Critical interlock:** wired so it physically cannot run unless the rear ramp is up AND the door sensors confirm sealed. Ozone is toxic to animals. This must be foolproof.

### 6. Antimicrobial coating on all interior surfaces
Silver-ion based, reapplied every 6 months. Bacteria are what actually create most of the lingering smell. Kill the bacteria, the smell never starts.

### 7. Built-in wash port
A 1.5" garden hose quick-connect on the outside of the van feeds an interior sprayer wand. 30 seconds of rinse and the whole interior is wet. Everything drains to the catch tank. You can wash the inside of this van in less time than it takes to brush a horse.

### 8. Hay nets only — no floor hay
Hay on the floor + urine = the single worst smell in horse hauling. Two nets per stall, mounted at chest height. Mini doesn't have to lift its head awkwardly; floor stays clean.

### 9. 12V dehumidifier
Small Peltier unit mounted behind the cab divider. Runs overnight after a trip on solar/house battery. Holds the interior at 40–50% humidity. Damp = mold = amplified smell forever. Dry van = clean van.

### 10. The smell test is non-negotiable
Every trip ends with: scoop pellets → rinse pan → ozone cycle → vent. Twenty minutes of work. The van smells like clean rubber and cedar, not like a horse. That's the standard.

## 7. Who Builds It

Three real paths:

**Option A — Full custom horse trailer fabricator.** Hawk Trailers (Fort Madison, IA) and Featherlite (Cresco, IA) both do Sprinter/Transit conversions. They know horses. You ship them the chassis and pick it up done. 4–6 month lead time, all-in cost lands at the upper end of the budget.

**Option B — Split the build.** Contract a North Idaho or Spokane van/RV converter for the box-out (floor, walls, electrical, ventilation, smell system, ramps) and ship the chassis to a horse trailer specialist for stalls, dividers, bars. Saves ~15%, takes longer to coordinate, two warranties.

**Option C — Local builder, oversight by you.** A North Idaho fab shop or boatbuilder can do this whole thing if you give them this spec and stay on it. Cheapest path, biggest risk on quality if you don't watch the floor and drain work like a hawk. The floor is the build — if it leaks, the whole thing is ruined.

**Recommendation:** Option A for the first build. Document it well. If this works as a business asset, build the second one cheaper later.

## 9. Open Questions Before Building

1. **Do we want a third stall option?** Sliding divider could let it do "3 small minis" if you keep adding minis to the herd.
2. **Heated interior?** North Idaho winters. A 12V diesel heater (Webasto-style) is $800–$1,200 installed. Worth it for shoulder-season events.
3. **Generator backup?** If the solar plus battery can't hold the fans at a multi-day event, a small inverter generator in a vented compartment is the fallback.
4. **Branding final call.** Matte black is the recommendation. Walnut wrap is the alternative — warmer, more "Tribal Cowboy," but harder to keep clean.
