## BRIEF FOR DESIGNER OR STACIE

Visual direction overall: Clean and credible. Each slide should feel like it belongs in a teacher's resource list, not a birthday party ad. Use cream or warm white backgrounds with gold and deep green type. The pony can appear in 1–2 slides. Avoid party-balloon energy — this is curriculum, not entertainment. Every slide with a standards callout should show the standard ID in a small, readable label (like a pull quote chip or subtle badge).

## SLIDES

---

### SLIDE 1 — COVER / HOOK

**Headline text (large, bold):**
A real pony walks into your classroom.
Four weeks later, your kids are readers.

**Subhead (smaller):**
The Pony Post — a free Pre-K/K literacy program.
Tribal Cowboy LLC. North Idaho.

**Visual direction:**
A photo of a pony standing calmly at the front of a classroom or at a school doorway — ideally with a child or two looking up at it in total fascination. If no classroom photo exists yet, use a clean outdoor shot of the pony with a child and overlay the text on a warm cream panel. The image should anchor left or top; text panel anchors right or bottom.

**Note:** The word "free" is doing a lot of work here. Keep it in. Principals get pitched constantly. This stops the scroll because the combination of "pony" + "classroom" + "free" + "readers" is specific enough to earn a swipe.

---

### SLIDE 2 — WHAT THE PROGRAM IS

**Headline:**
One visit per week. Same classroom. Same pony, different story each time.

**Body text:**
The Pony Post runs four weeks straight — one themed literacy visit per week, plus a letter from the pony delivered between each visit. Every letter recaps what we did, introduces a new vocabulary word, and drops a clue about next week's theme.

Kids don't know what's coming. That's the point.

**Visual direction:**
A simple illustrated or designed graphic: four numbered weeks in a horizontal row, connected by dotted lines (like a mail route). Clean. No clip art. This can be built in Canva with the Tribal Cowboy gold and teal palette.

---

### SLIDE 3 — WHAT EVERY CHILD FINISHES WITH

**Headline:**
Every child leaves the four-week Sprint with something they made.

**Body text:**
Over four weeks, each kid builds their own Pony Post Adventure Book — a paper activity book they decorate, stamp, write in, and personalize. Four Pony Post letters to keep. Themed take-homes from each visit.

What they carry out the door is theirs because they made it.

**Standards callout chip (overlay or below body text):**
Idaho ELA K.L.6 — Vocabulary acquisition through context
Idaho ELA K.RF.1.d — Letter-sound correspondence
Idaho Early Learning eGuidelines — Communication-Language-Literacy domain

**Visual direction:**
A flat-lay or overhead photo of an open activity book with a child's drawings, stamps, and handwriting inside it. Warm, lived-in, real. If this photo doesn't exist yet, substitute a close-up of hands stamping or writing — show the making, not just the product.

---

### SLIDE 4 — STANDARDS ALIGNMENT

**Headline:**
Built on what the research actually says.

**Body text:**
The Pony Post is designed around:

- The National Reading Panel's 5 Pillars of Reading
- Scarborough's Reading Rope (language comprehension + word recognition)
- National Early Literacy Panel predictors for kindergarten success

It aligns to 19 Idaho Kindergarten ELA standards and the Idaho Early Learning eGuidelines Communication-Language-Literacy domain.

This is an educational literacy program, not a field trip substitute.

**Visual direction:**
Clean white or cream slide. No photo needed here — let the list breathe. Consider using the Tribal Cowboy gold accent color on the bullet leaders or a thin left-border rule. Keep the type clean and readable at carousel size. This slide will get screenshotted by curriculum coordinators — design it accordingly.

---

### SLIDE 5 — THE LOCAL HISTORY WEEK (PIP)

**Headline:**
One week, the pony has a name tied to this place.

**Body text:**
Week three introduces Pip — named for the real pit ponies who worked Idaho's Silver Valley silver mines. Kids learn what those ponies did, why they mattered, and what mining life looked like for the families who lived it.

North Idaho history is part of the curriculum, not an add-on.

**Standards callout chip:**
Idaho ELA K.RI.3 — Key details in informational text
Idaho ELA K.SL.2 — Asking and answering questions about a read-aloud

**Visual direction:**
A split or layered slide — the pony on one side, and on the other side a simple illustrated or sepia-toned reference to mining (a lantern, a small cart, a helmet). Tribal Cowboy teal works well here as a color bridge between the two halves. Keep it warm, not dark. This isn't a history lecture — it's a story.

**Note to Stacie:** This slide is your competitive differentiator with any North Idaho principal. No other literacy program is teaching Silver Valley mining history through a live pony visit. Lead with it in any in-person pitch.

### SLIDE 6 — HOW IT WORKS FOR YOUR CLASSROOM

**Headline:**
What the teacher actually has to do: not much.

**Body text:**
Tribal Cowboy handles the pony, the materials, the letters, and the themed activity books. The teacher hands out envelopes between visits and keeps the book in progress.

No prep kits. No assembly required. One conversation at the start and we take it from there.

The program runs at no cost to the school. A local business sponsor covers everything.

**Visual direction:**
Simple icon-row or checklist layout: pony icon, envelope icon, activity book icon, sponsor badge icon — each with a one-line label. Keep it scannable. This is the "how easy is this actually" slide that gets saved and forwarded to principals.
